P2269

Water in Fuel Condition

Severity
High
Driving
Short journeys only
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering - injector circuits
Important. Water in a modern diesel fuel system is genuinely urgent. High-pressure pumps and injectors are lubricated by the fuel itself, and water destroys that lubrication - damage can spread from the pump through every injector, which is one of the most expensive failures a diesel can have. Draining the trap takes minutes; the alternative does not.

Water has been detected in the fuel. This is not a sensor complaint - the module is reporting that the water trap has collected enough water to matter. In a modern diesel that needs dealing with now rather than at the next service, because the fuel is what lubricates the high-pressure pump and the injectors.

Possible causes

  • CommonWater genuinely collected in the fuel filter or water trap
  • CommonWater trap never drained at service
  • CommonFailed water in fuel sensor
  • CommonCorroded connector - the sensor sits in or near the fuel filter housing
  • PossibleContaminated fuel from a poor-quality supply
  • PossibleCondensation from repeatedly running the tank very low

What you might notice

  • Water in fuel warning
  • Rough running or misfire
  • Hard starting
  • Loss of power
  • Check engine light on
  • Often no symptom at first

What to check, in order

  1. Drain the water trap. On most diesels this is a drain plug or tap at the bottom of the fuel filter housing, and it is a five-minute job described in the handbook.
  2. If the vehicle has been misfuelled, or a large amount of water is suspected, do not start the engine at all. Running the pump is what spreads contamination through the whole system.
  3. Change the fuel filter after draining - the element will have collected contamination as well as water.
  4. Ask where the vehicle was last fuelled. A single bad tank explains this, and returning to the same supplier repeats it.
  5. Keep the tank reasonably full in cold weather. Condensation forms in the air space above the fuel, so a habitually near-empty tank collects more water.
