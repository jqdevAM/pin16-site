P2258

Secondary Air Injection System Control "A" Circuit High

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering - injector circuits
Important. A pump that will not stop is worth attending to promptly. It is not designed for continuous duty, it flattens the battery, and the constant air into the exhaust makes the engine run richer than it should - which over time is hard on the catalytic converter.

Secondary air injection control circuit "A" is reading high - typically shorted to voltage, or a relay that will not release. This one matters more than the low variant, because a circuit stuck high can leave the pump running when it should have stopped. A pump designed to run for a minute at a time does not tolerate running continuously.

Possible causes

  • CommonRelay contacts welded closed
  • CommonWiring shorted to voltage
  • CommonCorroded connector bridging pins
  • PossibleChafed harness against a supply wire
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Pump running continuously, or long after a cold start
  • Loud buzzing from the engine bay that does not stop
  • Check engine light on
  • Battery draining
  • Worse fuel economy

What to check, in order

  1. Listen for the pump. If it is still running once the engine is warm, remove the relay to stop it while you diagnose - a pump running continuously overheats and destroys itself.
  2. Check whether the high reading persists with the relay removed.
  3. Swap the relay. Welded contacts are the most common cause.
  4. Inspect the connector for corrosion bridging pins, and the harness for a chafe point against a supply wire.
  5. Check whether the fuel trims have shifted. Air pumped into the exhaust continuously makes the oxygen sensors read lean, so the module adds fuel it does not need.
