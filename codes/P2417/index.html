P2417

O2 Sensor Signals Swapped Bank 2 Sensor 2 / Bank 2 Sensor 3

Severity
Low
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Auxiliary emission controls

The module has worked out that the signals from sensors 2 and 3 on bank 2 have been swapped over. Each sits at a different point along the exhaust and should therefore behave differently - the one nearer the engine responds faster and runs hotter - so when those behaviours arrive on the wrong wires, the module can tell. This is a wiring mistake far more often than a component failure, and it usually appears immediately after exhaust or sensor work.

Possible causes

  • CommonThe two sensor connectors plugged into each other's harness after recent work
  • CommonSensors fitted into each other's positions in the exhaust
  • PossibleRepair or extension harness wired the wrong way round
  • PossibleAftermarket sensors whose connectors allow a mistake the originals prevented
  • RareGenuine sensor fault making one behave like the other

What you might notice

  • Check engine light on
  • Failed emissions test
  • Catalyst efficiency codes that do not make sense
  • Usually no change in how the car drives

What to check, in order

  1. Find out what was done to the exhaust recently. This code rarely appears on a vehicle nobody has touched, and the answer is usually in the last job rather than in the wiring diagram.
  2. Trace both sensor harnesses physically from sensor to connector. They often run close together and are easy to cross, particularly where they pass behind a heat shield.
  3. Check the sensors are in the right holes as well as on the right plugs. Fitting them into each other's positions produces the same code as crossing the connectors.
  4. Do not simply clear this and move on. If the sensors really are crossed, the module is making fuelling and catalyst decisions on inverted information.
