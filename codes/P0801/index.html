P0801

Reverse Inhibit Control Circuit

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in

A fault in the reverse inhibit control circuit, which blocks reverse being selected when it would damage the transmission - typically above a certain road speed. Depending on which way the fault goes, reverse may not engage, may engage when it should not, or may engage without the rest of the car knowing about it - so reversing lamps, parking sensors and the reversing camera can all fail to come on.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed or misadjusted reverse switch, or the transmission range sensor
  • CommonWiring or connector fault on the circuit
  • CommonCorroded connector at the transmission - it sits low and takes road spray
  • PossibleBlown fuse on the reversing lamp circuit
  • PossibleFailed reverse inhibit solenoid on vehicles that use one
  • PossibleAftermarket parking sensors or a camera spliced into the circuit

What you might notice

  • Reversing lamps not working, or staying on
  • Reversing camera or parking sensors not activating
  • Reverse will not engage
  • Reverse engaging harshly
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Check whether the reversing lamps work with reverse selected. This one observation separates a switch or wiring fault from a transmission problem, with no tools.
  2. Watch the selected gear on the live data screen while selecting reverse.
  3. Check the transmission range sensor adjustment - it reports reverse on most vehicles, so a sensor fault produces this code too.
  4. Inspect the connector at the transmission for corrosion.
  5. Ask whether aftermarket parking sensors, a camera or a tow bar have been fitted. These are commonly spliced into the reverse circuit.
  6. If reverse itself will not engage, check the fluid level and condition before assuming an electrical fault.
