P0665

Intake Manifold Tuning Valve Control Circuit High (Bank 2)

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Computer output circuits and control module
Important. The intake manifold tuning system changes the effective length of the intake tract, giving good torque low down and good breathing higher up. When it stops working the engine still runs - it just loses one or other end of its power delivery.

A fault in the intake manifold tuning valve control circuit on bank 2 - a high reading, usually a short to voltage or a lost earth. The module cannot switch the tuning valve, so the intake stays in one configuration and the engine loses part of its power delivery.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed intake manifold tuning valve or its solenoid
  • CommonLinkage or flap seized with carbon
  • CommonSplit, perished or disconnected vacuum hose
  • CommonCorroded connector or damaged wiring
  • PossibleBroken flap or linkage inside the manifold
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Loss of low-speed torque, or loss of top-end power
  • Engine feeling flat in part of its rev range
  • Rough idle
  • Worse fuel economy
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Check the vacuum hoses first on a vacuum-operated system. A split or disconnected hose is the most common cause and the cheapest to fix.
  2. Check the flap or linkage moves freely. Carbon seizing the mechanism is very common on a high-mileage engine, particularly a diesel used for short journeys.
  3. Measure the solenoid winding resistance against specification.
  4. Inspect the connector for corrosion.
  5. Be aware that a broken flap inside the manifold can be drawn into the engine on some designs. If the linkage feels loose or disconnected, have it inspected rather than ignored.
