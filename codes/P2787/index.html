P2787

Clutch Temperature Too High

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Transmission
Important. An overheating clutch can leave you unable to move at all until it cools, which is worth knowing before it happens on a hill in traffic. Stopping to let it cool is the correct response, not a workaround.

The clutch is overheating. On an automated manual or dual-clutch transmission the clutch is slipped by the module to move the car away from rest, and doing that repeatedly - in traffic, on a hill, or manoeuvring - generates a great deal of heat. The transmission will protect itself by reducing power or refusing to move until it cools.

Possible causes

  • CommonRepeated hill starts or prolonged stop-start traffic
  • CommonHolding the vehicle on a slope using the transmission rather than the brakes
  • CommonWorn clutch slipping more than it should
  • PossibleClutch contaminated with oil from a leaking seal
  • PossibleTowing or a heavy load
  • PossibleClutch adaptation never run after a repair

What you might notice

  • Transmission warning message and a request to stop
  • Refusal to move
  • Burning smell
  • Judder when moving off
  • Reduced power

What to check, in order

  1. Stop somewhere safe and let it cool. Continuing puts the clutch through more of exactly what is damaging it.
  2. Use the brakes to hold the car on a slope, never the transmission. On these gearboxes that single habit is the difference between a clutch that lasts and one that does not.
  3. If there is a burning smell, the clutch is already being damaged - do not continue.
  4. Assess clutch wear, and look for oil contamination from a leaking crankshaft or gearbox seal.
  5. Run the clutch adaptation procedure and check whether it improves matters.
