P0210

Injector Circuit/Open - Cylinder 10

Severity
High
Driving
Short journeys only
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering - injector circuits
Important. A flashing check engine light means unburnt fuel is reaching the catalytic converter. Stop driving - a converter costs far more than an injector.

The injector circuit for cylinder 10 is open - a broken wire, a disconnected plug, or a failed injector winding. Cylinder 10 is getting no fuel, so it is not contributing and the engine will misfire.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed injector - open or shorted winding
  • CommonCorroded or damaged connector at the injector
  • CommonChafed or heat-damaged wiring in the injector harness
  • PossibleBlown fuse or failed relay on the injector supply
  • PossibleOil or fuel in the connector from a leaking seal
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Misfire - the engine shaking, especially at idle
  • Loss of power and hesitation
  • Hard starting or no start
  • Smell of unburnt fuel
  • Check engine light on, or flashing

What to check, in order

  1. Check the connector at the cylinder 10 injector is plugged in and locked. These get disturbed during plug changes and intake work.
  2. Read all codes. A misfire code for the same cylinder alongside this one confirms which cylinder is not firing.
  3. Measure the injector winding resistance and compare it with a known-good injector on the same engine. Comparing against its neighbours is often more useful than a specification.
  4. Swap the connector - not the injector - with a neighbouring cylinder and see whether the fault follows. If it does, the fault is in the injector; if it stays, it is in the wiring.
  5. Inspect the connector for corrosion, and for oil or fuel that has crept in through a failed seal.
  6. Check the injector harness for chafe and heat damage. It sits directly on top of a hot engine and gets disturbed at every service.
