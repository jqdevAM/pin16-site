P2130

Throttle/Pedal Position Sensor/Switch "F" Circuit

Severity
Critical
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering
Important. A vehicle that can enter reduced-power mode at any moment is a hazard when joining fast-moving traffic. Drive it to be repaired, not on a journey that depends on it.

A fault in the throttle or pedal position sensor "F" circuit. Modern vehicles use several of these sensors so that a single failure can be detected by disagreement, which is why one failing sensor produces a reduced-power mode rather than uncontrolled behaviour.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed throttle or pedal position sensor
  • CommonCorroded or damaged connector
  • CommonWiring damage, particularly at the pedal where feet catch the harness
  • CommonLoss of the 5 volt reference or the sensor earth
  • PossibleCarbon on the throttle plate affecting the reported position
  • PossibleSensor not calibrated after replacement

What you might notice

  • Reduced power mode
  • Hesitation or surging
  • Engine not responding to the pedal
  • Rough or unstable idle
  • Warning light for reduced engine power

What to check, in order

  1. Watch all the pedal and throttle position readings together on the live data screen while pressing the pedal slowly through its full travel. The one that jumps, sticks or does not follow the others is the fault.
  2. Check the reference voltage and earth at the sensor. Several sensors usually share these, so a lost reference produces a scatter of codes from healthy sensors.
  3. Inspect the connector for corrosion and water, and the harness at the pedal for damage.
  4. Look for other codes sharing the same reference circuit before condemning this sensor.
  5. On most vehicles the pedal sensors are part of the pedal assembly and are not supplied separately - establish that before ordering.
