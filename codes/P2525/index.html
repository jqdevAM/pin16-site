P2525

Vacuum Reservoir Pressure Sensor Circuit

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs
Important. On many vehicles the vacuum reservoir feeds the brake servo. If the brake pedal feels harder than normal, treat this as urgent rather than routine - the brakes still work, but they need far more effort, and that is not something to discover in an emergency stop.

A fault in the vacuum reservoir pressure sensor circuit. A vacuum reservoir stores vacuum so it is available when the engine is not making much - at full throttle, or on a diesel, which produces very little. On many vehicles that store feeds the brake servo.

Possible causes

  • CommonSplit, perished or disconnected vacuum hose
  • CommonFailed non-return valve on the reservoir
  • CommonFailed vacuum pump, on engines that use one
  • CommonFailed pressure sensor
  • PossibleCorroded connector or damaged wiring
  • PossibleCracked or corroded reservoir

What you might notice

  • Brake pedal harder than usual
  • More pedal effort needed to stop
  • Hissing from the pedal area
  • Vacuum-operated actuators not working
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Test the brake pedal before driving. With the engine off, press it several times to use up the stored vacuum, then start the engine with your foot on the pedal - it should sink slightly as assistance arrives. If it does not, assistance is genuinely missing.
  2. Check the vacuum hoses and the non-return valve for splits, looseness and perishing.
  3. On a diesel or a direct-injection petrol engine, check the vacuum pump - these engines make little vacuum of their own and depend on it.
  4. Inspect the sensor connector for corrosion.
  5. Check the reference voltage and earth at the sensor.
