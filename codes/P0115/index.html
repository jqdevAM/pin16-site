P0115

Engine Coolant Temperature Circuit

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering

A fault in the engine coolant temperature sensor circuit. This reading matters more than most people expect: the module uses it to decide how much extra fuel to add when cold, when to enable closed-loop fuelling, and when to run the cooling fans. A wrong reading has knock-on effects across all of those.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed coolant temperature sensor
  • CommonDamaged wiring or connector at the sensor
  • PossibleLow coolant level leaving the sensor out of the coolant
  • PossibleCorrosion in the connector

What you might notice

  • Check engine light on
  • Worse fuel economy
  • Cooling fans running constantly or not at all
  • Harder starting
  • Temperature gauge reading implausibly

What to check, in order

  1. Check the coolant level first - a sensor sitting in air rather than coolant reads wrongly through no fault of its own.
  2. Compare the coolant temperature reading on the live data screen against the gauge and against intake air temperature on a cold engine. On a cold car all should read close to ambient.
  3. Inspect the connector for corrosion and the wiring for chafing.
  4. Replace the sensor once wiring and coolant level are eliminated.
