P3450

Cylinder 7 Deactivation/Intake Valve Control Performance

Severity
High
Driving
Short journeys only
System
Powertrain
Group
Auxiliary emission controls
Important. A flashing check engine light means the misfire is severe enough to damage the catalytic converter. Stop and have the vehicle recovered rather than driving on.

The intake valve control for cylinder 7 is electrically sound but not doing what it was told - the valve is not being held or released as commanded. This is the mechanical side of cylinder deactivation, and on a high-mileage engine it usually means the special lifter for cylinder 7 is failing. That matters more than a circuit fault, because a collapsing lifter can go on to damage the camshaft lobe it runs against.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed or collapsed deactivation lifter on cylinder 7
  • CommonLow, old or wrong-viscosity engine oil
  • CommonBlocked oil passage to the cylinder 7 lifter
  • CommonWeak or sticking control solenoid
  • PossibleLow oil pressure from a worn pump or clogged pickup screen
  • PossibleWorn camshaft lobe for cylinder 7

What you might notice

  • Misfire on cylinder 7
  • Ticking or tapping from the top of the engine
  • Shudder as the system switches in and out
  • Reduced power
  • Check engine light on, sometimes flashing

What to check, in order

  1. Check the oil level, condition and viscosity first. A hydraulically actuated lifter cannot work on oil that is low, degraded or too thick, and this is the most common cause by a wide margin.
  2. Listen for a tick from the top of the engine. A persistent tick alongside this code points firmly at the cylinder 7 lifter rather than the solenoid.
  3. Read all codes. A misfire code for cylinder 7 alongside this one supports a mechanical fault.
  4. Check oil pressure against specification.
  5. Do not keep driving on a persistent misfire. Unburnt fuel reaching the catalytic converter destroys it, and a collapsing lifter can wear the camshaft lobe - either one turns this into a far more expensive repair.
  6. If the engine has done high mileage on extended oil changes, expect the repair to involve lifters rather than a solenoid, and get a proper assessment before spending on parts.
