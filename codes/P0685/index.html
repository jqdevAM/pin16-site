P0685

ECM/PCM Power Relay Control Circuit/Open

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Computer output circuits and control module
Important. If the battery goes flat overnight, investigate this rather than replacing the battery. A relay that will not release keeps the module and everything it feeds powered up all night.

A fault in the control circuit for the engine control module's own power relay - an open circuit, usually a broken wire or a failed relay winding. This is the relay the module uses to switch its own main supply. If it cannot be controlled, the module may lose power entirely, or may keep power after the car is switched off, which flattens the battery overnight.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed relay
  • CommonCorroded relay socket or fuse box connections
  • CommonWiring fault between the module and the relay
  • CommonWater ingress into the fuse box
  • PossibleBlown fuse
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Engine will not start
  • Engine cutting out
  • Battery flat after standing
  • Cooling fan or fuel pump running with the ignition off
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Swap the relay with an identical one from elsewhere in the box and see whether the fault follows it. It costs nothing and is the fastest test available.
  2. Inspect the relay socket for corrosion, heat damage and spread terminals - a discoloured socket means the relay has been running hot.
  3. Check the fuse for the circuit.
  4. Check the fuse box for water ingress, particularly if it is mounted low or in the engine bay.
  5. Check the wiring between the module and the relay.
