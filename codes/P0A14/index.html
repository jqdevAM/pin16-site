P0A14

Engine Mount Control Circuit/Open

Severity
Low
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Hybrid propulsion

The control circuit for an electronically controlled engine mount is open - a broken wire, a disconnected plug, or a failed mount solenoid. These mounts change their stiffness on command, and on a hybrid they earn their keep at the moment the engine starts and stops while the car is moving: a soft mount absorbs the shudder that would otherwise reach the cabin. With the mount uncontrolled it reverts to a fixed state, so the car becomes rougher rather than unsafe. This is a comfort fault in a system full of serious ones, and it is rated accordingly.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed mount solenoid or actuator
  • CommonCorroded or damaged connector at the mount
  • CommonWiring chafed where it crosses the engine, which moves constantly
  • CommonVacuum line split or disconnected, on vacuum-actuated mounts
  • PossibleBlown fuse
  • RareFailed driver inside the control module

What you might notice

  • Vibration through the cabin, worst at idle
  • A noticeable shudder as the engine starts or stops
  • Booming or droning at certain engine speeds
  • Check engine light on
  • No effect on power or on how the car drives otherwise

What to check, in order

  1. Inspect the connector and the wiring at the mount. It sits on a component that moves under every gearchange, so chafed wiring is the usual answer.
  2. On a vacuum-actuated mount, check the vacuum line for splits and for a line that has come off. This is common and costs nothing to fix.
  3. Measure the solenoid's resistance against specification.
  4. Check the mount's physical condition while you are there. A mount whose rubber has collapsed produces the same vibration and will not be fixed electrically.
  5. Do not disturb anything orange.
