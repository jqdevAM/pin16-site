P0637

Power Steering Control Circuit High

Severity
Critical
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Computer output circuits and control module
Important. Losing power steering assistance is one of the few faults that becomes dangerous within seconds rather than eventually. If the steering suddenly feels heavy, stop somewhere safe rather than driving on to see whether it recovers.

A fault in the power steering control circuit - a high reading, usually a short to voltage or a lost earth. On a vehicle with electric or electrically assisted steering, this is the circuit that controls the assistance. If it fails, the steering becomes very heavy - the car remains steerable, but it takes far more effort than the driver expects, and that is most noticeable at parking speeds.

Possible causes

  • CommonWiring or connector fault on the control circuit
  • CommonCorroded connector at the power steering unit
  • CommonFailed power steering control module or motor
  • CommonLow battery voltage - electric steering draws heavily and drops out on a weak supply
  • PossibleBlown fuse or fusible link on the steering supply
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Steering much heavier than normal
  • Power steering warning light
  • Assistance coming and going
  • Heavy steering worst when parking
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. If the steering has already gone heavy, stop somewhere safe rather than continuing. Assistance that has dropped out once can drop out again.
  2. Check battery voltage and the terminals. Electric power steering draws a large current and is one of the first things to drop out on a weak supply.
  3. Check the fuse or fusible link for the steering circuit.
  4. Inspect the connector at the steering unit for corrosion and water.
  5. Read all codes - a steering module code alongside this one points at the unit rather than the circuit.
