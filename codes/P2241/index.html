P2241

O2 Sensor Positive Current Control Circuit Low (Bank 2 Sensor 1)

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering - injector circuits

A fault in the positive current control circuit for the upstream oxygen sensor on bank 2 - a low reading, usually a short to earth or an open circuit. A wideband oxygen sensor is not a simple voltage generator: the module passes a small current through a pump cell to hold the sensor at a fixed point, and measures the current needed. These are the wires carrying that current, so a fault here stops the sensor reporting at all.

Possible causes

  • CommonCorroded or water-ingressed connector at the sensor
  • CommonWiring damaged by exhaust heat or chafing
  • CommonFailed wideband oxygen sensor
  • PossibleAftermarket or wrong-specification sensor fitted
  • PossibleLoss of reference or earth
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Check engine light on
  • Worse fuel economy
  • Failed emissions test
  • Fuel trim stuck at a default value
  • Rough running in some conditions

What to check, in order

  1. Inspect the connector at the sensor. It lives in the hottest part of the exhaust and its connector fails more often than the element does.
  2. Check the harness where it passes near the exhaust for heat and chafe damage.
  3. Ask whether a non-original sensor was fitted. Wideband sensors are calibrated to the vehicle and a cheap substitute frequently sets exactly these codes.
  4. Check both banks. Both faulting together points at a shared supply or earth rather than two failed sensors.
  5. Clear the wiring before buying a sensor - wideband sensors are expensive.
