P0656

Fuel Level Output Circuit

Severity
Low
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Computer output circuits and control module

A fault in the fuel level output circuit. The module cannot pass the fuel level on to whatever uses it - the gauge, the range calculation, or the misfire detection logic that needs to know the tank is not nearly empty. A very low tank causes misfires that are not faults, which is why the module wants this reading.

Possible causes

  • CommonWiring or connector fault on the output circuit
  • CommonCorroded connector at either end
  • PossibleFault in the module receiving the signal, loading the circuit
  • PossibleChafed harness shorting against the body or engine
  • PossibleAftermarket equipment spliced into the circuit
  • RareFailed control module output driver
  • CommonFaulty fuel level sender in the tank

What you might notice

  • Fuel gauge wrong or dead
  • Range calculation implausible
  • Misfire codes appearing when the tank is nearly empty
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Check whether the fuel gauge reads correctly. A wrong gauge points at the sender in the tank rather than at this output circuit.
  2. Inspect the connectors and wiring.
  3. Note that running consistently on a very low tank causes genuine misfires as the pump draws air, and can shorten the fuel pump's life.
