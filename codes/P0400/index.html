P0400

Exhaust Gas Recirculation Flow

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Auxiliary emission controls
Important. EGR is emissions control equipment. Removing the valve or fitting a blanking plate to silence the code is an offence in the EU, the UK and the US, and will fail a periodic emissions inspection. Repair it rather than defeat it.

The exhaust gas recirculation system is not behaving as the control module expects. EGR deliberately feeds a small amount of inert exhaust gas back into the intake to lower combustion temperature and cut nitrogen oxide emissions. This is the general code for that system being wrong without the module committing to whether flow is too high or too low - a more specific code alongside it will usually say which.

Possible causes

  • CommonCarbon build-up in the EGR valve or its passages
  • CommonEGR valve stuck open or stuck shut
  • PossibleFaulty EGR position or differential pressure sensor
  • PossibleVacuum leak or collapsed vacuum hose on a vacuum-operated valve
  • PossibleFaulty EGR control solenoid
  • PossibleWiring fault between the module and the EGR actuator

What you might notice

  • Check engine light on
  • Failed emissions test
  • Rough idle
  • Pinging or knocking under load
  • Often no symptom the driver notices

What to check, in order

  1. Read all stored codes before starting. This code on its own is vague, but P0401 (too little flow) or P0402 (too much) alongside it points straight at the problem.
  2. Inspect the EGR valve and its mounting passage for carbon. Carbon is the cause in the large majority of EGR faults on a high-mileage engine.
  3. On a vacuum-operated valve, check the vacuum hose is intact and that vacuum actually reaches the valve when the module commands it.
  4. Watch the commanded EGR position against the actual position on the live data screen. A large persistent gap means the valve is not moving as instructed.
  5. Check the wiring and connector at the valve for corrosion or heat damage. The valve sits in a hot part of the engine bay and its connector suffers for it.
