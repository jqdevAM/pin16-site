P2711

Unexpected Mechanical Gear Disengagement

Severity
Critical
Driving
Stop driving
System
Powertrain
Group
Transmission
Important. Unexpected disengagement means the car can stop driving in the middle of a manoeuvre. Have it recovered - this is one of the few transmission faults where continuing to drive puts you in traffic without drive.

The transmission came out of gear on its own. The module commanded a gear, the transmission was in it, and then it was not - without being asked. That means drive can disappear without warning, and it can happen at any speed: pulling out of a junction, overtaking, or climbing a hill in traffic.

Possible causes

  • CommonWorn clutch pack or band no longer holding
  • CommonLow or degraded transmission fluid
  • CommonLow line pressure - worn pump, blocked filter, failed pressure solenoid
  • CommonSticking valve in the valve body releasing pressure unexpectedly
  • PossibleInternal seal or drum failure
  • PossibleSelector or parking mechanism fault on some designs
  • PossibleFaulty speed sensor making the module believe drive was lost

What you might notice

  • Sudden loss of drive while moving
  • Engine revving with no forward motion
  • A bang or lurch as drive disappears or returns
  • Transmission dropping into neutral
  • Burnt smell from the fluid

What to check, in order

  1. Stop driving. Losing drive while overtaking or pulling into traffic is a genuine hazard, and this code says it has already happened once.
  2. Check the fluid level and condition first. It is the cheapest check and it explains more transmission complaints than every electrical fault combined. Check for clutch material while you are there.
  3. Read all codes. Ratio, pressure or solenoid codes alongside this one identify what let go.
  4. Have the vehicle recovered rather than driven to a garage. This is not a fault that gives warning before it repeats.
  5. A line pressure test is the way to separate a hydraulic cause from a worn clutch, and it is worth doing before any parts are bought.
