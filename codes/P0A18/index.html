P0A18

Motor Torque Sensor Circuit Range/Performance

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Hybrid propulsion
Important. High-voltage hazard nearby. The transaxle on a hybrid contains high-voltage components and orange cabling. Reading codes and checking connectors on low-voltage circuits is safe; do not disturb anything orange.

The drive motor torque sensor is answering, but the value is not plausible against what the module knows from motor speed, road speed and load. Either the sensor is inaccurate, or the motor is genuinely not producing the torque that was commanded - and the second possibility is the one worth ruling out first, because it points at the inverter or the traction battery rather than at a cheap sensor.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailing or drifting torque sensor
  • CommonHigh resistance in the wiring skewing the reading
  • CommonFault in the drive motor or inverter itself
  • PossibleTraction battery degraded, limiting what the drive can deliver
  • PossibleLow 12 volt battery voltage
  • RareFailed control module input

What you might notice

  • Reduced power
  • Hybrid or electric drive system warning message
  • Engine and electric drive not blending smoothly
  • Reduced range, or the battery state of charge behaving oddly
  • Acceleration that varies between journeys

What to check, in order

  1. Check the 12 volt battery first - it is the most common cause of scattered hybrid system codes.
  2. Read all codes. Inverter or traction battery codes alongside this one mean the sensor is probably reporting a real problem rather than having one.
  3. Check the wiring and connector for high resistance.
  4. Have the traction battery state of health assessed if the vehicle has significant mileage - a degraded battery genuinely limits what the drive can produce, and the sensor is then telling the truth.
  5. Do not disturb anything orange.
