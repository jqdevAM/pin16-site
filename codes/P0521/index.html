P0521

Engine Oil Pressure Sensor/Switch Range/Performance

Severity
High
Driving
Short journeys only
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs

The oil pressure sensor is answering, but the pressure it reports is not plausible - it does not change with engine speed the way it should, or it sits at an unlikely value. Either the sensor is wrong, or the pressure genuinely is, and the second possibility is the one that matters.

Possible causes

  • CommonLow engine oil level
  • CommonFailed oil pressure sensor or switch
  • CommonCorroded or oil-contaminated connector at the sensor
  • CommonWiring damage - the sensor sits low on a hot engine
  • PossibleOil of the wrong grade, or long overdue for changing
  • PossibleWorn oil pump, blocked pickup screen or worn bearings

What you might notice

  • Oil pressure warning light
  • Gauge reading zero or implausibly high
  • Ticking or rumbling from the engine
  • Check engine light on
  • Often no symptom if only the sensor has failed

What to check, in order

  1. Fit a mechanical gauge and measure the real pressure at idle and at raised engine speed. Everything else here is guesswork until you have that number.
  2. Check the oil level first. It costs nothing, it is the most common cause, and it is the one thing you must not skip.
  3. Inspect the connector at the sensor for oil contamination - a leaking sensor seal fills its own plug and produces an electrical fault from a mechanical leak.
  4. Check the wiring for chafe and heat damage.
  5. Confirm the actual pressure with a mechanical gauge before dismissing this as a sensor fault. That measurement is the only thing that separates a lying sensor from a dying engine.
