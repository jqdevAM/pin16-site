P0553

Power Steering Pressure Sensor/Switch Circuit High Input

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs

The power steering pressure signal is reading high - typically shorted to voltage or with a lost earth, though a restriction in the system will genuinely raise pressure.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed power steering pressure sensor or switch
  • CommonLow power steering fluid, on a hydraulic system
  • CommonCorroded connector or damaged wiring
  • PossibleLeaking power steering hose or pump
  • PossibleLoss of reference voltage or earth

What you might notice

  • Engine stalling or dipping when the steering is turned at low speed
  • Rough idle when parking
  • Heavy steering
  • Whining from the power steering pump
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Check the power steering fluid level and look for leaks. A hydraulic pump low on fluid loads the engine unpredictably and whines while doing it.
  2. Watch the sensor reading while someone turns the steering from lock to lock with the engine idling - the pressure should rise clearly.
  3. Inspect the connector for corrosion and fluid contamination.
  4. Check the reference voltage and earth at the sensor.
  5. Note what this sensor is for: it tells the module to raise idle speed when the steering pump loads the engine. That is why the symptom is stalling while parking rather than anything to do with the steering itself.
