P0561

System Voltage Unstable

Severity
High
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs
Important. An unstable supply is the most common cause of a vehicle with many unrelated fault codes and no obvious single problem. Fix it first, clear everything, and see what actually comes back.

The system voltage is unstable - it is moving around rather than sitting where it should. Modules read sensors by comparing them against their supply, so an unsteady supply makes steady sensors look erratic. This is one of the more useful codes to find, because it explains a great deal of otherwise baffling behaviour elsewhere in the vehicle.

Possible causes

  • CommonLoose or corroded battery terminal
  • CommonCorroded earth strap
  • CommonFailing alternator with worn brushes or a failing regulator
  • CommonSlipping drive belt
  • PossibleBattery on the point of failing
  • PossibleFailing diode in the alternator producing ripple
  • PossibleChafed or fatigued supply wiring

What you might notice

  • Lights flickering or pulsing
  • Warning lights coming and going
  • Erratic gauge readings
  • Radio or displays resetting
  • Several unrelated codes stored at once

What to check, in order

  1. Watch voltage on the live data screen while wiggling the battery terminals and earth straps with the engine running. A reading that jumps has located the fault.
  2. Check the drive belt. A belt slipping on the alternator pulley produces exactly this, and it is often audible as a squeal on a cold start or under load.
  3. Have the alternator tested for ripple as well as for output. A failed diode passes the output test but makes the supply unsteady.
  4. Perform a voltage drop test on the supply and earth circuits under load.
  5. Have the battery load tested. A failing battery cannot smooth the supply and produces instability that looks like an alternator fault.
  6. Read all stored codes but do not chase them yet. Most will disappear when the supply is stable.
