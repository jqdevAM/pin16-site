U0320

Software Incompatibility with Power Steering Control Module

Severity
Critical
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Network and communication
Group
Network software
Important. Unlike a lost-communication fault, this does not usually take the assistance away mid-corner - the system simply never starts. That makes it less dangerous but no less urgent, because the car is genuinely hard to steer at low speed.

The software in the power steering control module is not compatible with the rest of the vehicle. On a car with electric power steering that module provides the assistance, and a version mismatch normally stops it initialising at all - so the steering will be very heavy from the moment you set off, rather than becoming heavy without warning while you are driving.

Possible causes

  • CommonThe power steering control module replaced but never programmed or coded to this vehicle
  • CommonUsed module fitted from a different vehicle, model year or specification
  • CommonSoftware update interrupted, often by battery voltage falling during programming
  • CommonOne module updated while others were left at their old versions
  • CommonWrong part number fitted - steering modules vary by market and specification
  • RareCorrupted module software

What you might notice

  • Steering very heavy from the moment you move off
  • Power steering warning light on
  • Steering assistance not available at all
  • Fault present since a repair or service visit

What to check, in order

  1. Do not drive it far. Heavy steering is manageable at speed but very difficult in a car park or at a junction, and the effort needed catches people out.
  2. Ask what was done to the vehicle recently - a steering rack, column or module replacement immediately before this appeared is the answer.
  3. Read the module's software and part numbers and compare them against the vehicle's build specification.
  4. Check whether the part number matches the vehicle's market and specification. Steering modules are calibrated to the car's weight and steering geometry, so a physically identical unit from a different variant will not do.
  5. Do not replace the module again. It is almost certainly the wrong software rather than the wrong hardware.
  6. This needs manufacturer-level programming. A generic scan tool cannot resolve it.
