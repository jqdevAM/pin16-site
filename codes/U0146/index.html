U0146

Lost Communication With Gateway "A"

Severity
High
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Network and communication
Group
Network communication
Important. Do not clear the other codes and start diagnosing them one by one. A missing gateway or body control module generates codes across the whole vehicle, and every one of them will disappear when this fault is fixed.

The control module has stopped receiving messages from gateway "A". This module sits at the centre of the vehicle's networks - a gateway passes messages between the separate buses, and a body control module runs much of the vehicle's electrical behaviour. When one of these goes quiet, everything that depended on it goes quiet too, so expect a long list of other fault codes that are consequences rather than separate problems.

Possible causes

  • CommonLow battery voltage or a discharged battery, dropping modules off the bus
  • CommonBlown fuse or corroded earth on the supply to gateway "A"
  • CommonDamaged, chafed or corroded bus wiring
  • CommonWater ingress at the module - these often sit low in the footwell or behind a wheel arch
  • PossibleFailed module
  • PossibleAftermarket electrical work spliced into the bus
  • PossibleModule not coded or configured after a previous repair or replacement

What you might notice

  • A large number of fault codes across unrelated systems
  • Several warning lights on at once
  • Scan tool cannot see whole groups of modules
  • Central locking, lighting, wipers or windows behaving strangely
  • Battery draining overnight
  • Vehicle may not start

What to check, in order

  1. Fix this before chasing anything else. When a gateway or body control module is missing, most of the other codes stored are symptoms of this one, and diagnosing them individually wastes a great deal of time.
  2. Check battery voltage and state of charge before anything else. A weak or failing battery drops modules off the bus and sets lost-communication codes with no wiring fault at all - this is the single most common false lead in this whole family.
  3. Check whether this code is current or historic. Lost-communication codes are routinely set by a flat battery, a jump start or a battery disconnection, and a historic code straight after one of those events usually records the event rather than a fault.
  4. Check the fuses and earth points for gateway "A".
  5. Look for water. These modules are commonly mounted in footwells, under seats or behind wheel arch liners, and a blocked drain or a leaking screen seal is a frequent cause.
  6. Ask what was done to the car recently. Aftermarket alarms, trackers and tow bar wiring spliced into the bus are a common cause.
  7. Record the full code list before clearing anything. After the repair, clear them and see which ones actually come back - usually far fewer than were stored.
