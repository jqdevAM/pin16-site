P0702

Transmission Control System Electrical

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Transmission

An electrical fault has been found in the transmission control system - the wiring, a connector, or a component circuit rather than the mechanical transmission. The module has usually dropped into a failsafe mode, which typically means one fixed gear and no shifting, so the car will move but very badly.

Possible causes

  • CommonDamaged or corroded transmission connector - the main case connector is a common failure point
  • CommonFailed internal wiring harness inside the transmission
  • CommonFluid wicking up the harness into the connector or the module
  • PossibleBlown fuse or poor earth on the transmission module supply
  • PossibleFailed solenoid or sensor
  • RareFailed transmission control module

What you might notice

  • Transmission stuck in one gear
  • No shifting at all
  • Harsh engagement
  • Several transmission codes stored together
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Read all transmission codes together. Several electrical codes at once point at a shared cause - the case connector, the internal harness or the power supply - not at several failed parts.
  2. Inspect the main transmission case connector. Pull it and look for fluid inside, corroded pins and green residue. Fluid wicking through the harness is a classic cause and it also destroys the module it reaches.
  3. Check the fuses and earths for the transmission module.
  4. Check the fluid level and condition.
  5. Do not order a control module until the connector and internal harness have been eliminated. Modules are expensive and usually innocent.
