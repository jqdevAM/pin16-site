P0638

Throttle Actuator Control Range/Performance (Bank 1)

Severity
Critical
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Computer output circuits and control module
Important. A vehicle that can enter reduced-power mode at any moment is a hazard when joining fast-moving traffic. Drive it to be repaired, not on a journey that depends on it.

The throttle actuator on bank 1 is not reaching the position the module commanded. The circuit is not being blamed - the throttle plate is physically not going where it was told, usually because carbon has made it stiff. The module responds by dropping the engine into a reduced-power mode, and that happens without warning.

Possible causes

  • CommonCarbon build-up making the throttle plate stiff
  • CommonFailing throttle actuator motor
  • CommonThrottle body never relearned after cleaning or replacement
  • PossibleFaulty throttle position sensor giving an inaccurate reading
  • PossibleHigh resistance in the wiring weakening the drive
  • PossibleVacuum leak downstream affecting the airflow the module expects

What you might notice

  • Sudden loss of power - reduced-power mode
  • Engine will not rev beyond a low limit
  • Rough or unstable idle
  • Warning light for reduced engine power
  • Hesitation

What to check, in order

  1. Be aware that power can be cut without warning. Avoid overtaking and motorway slip roads until this is fixed.
  2. Compare commanded against actual throttle position on the live data screen through a full sweep.
  3. Inspect the throttle body for carbon. On a drive-by-wire throttle, cleaning without running the relearn procedure afterwards can leave the idle worse than before - check whether a relearn is specified before starting.
  4. Check the wiring for high resistance before replacing the actuator.
  5. Read all codes - throttle position sensor codes alongside this one change the diagnosis.
