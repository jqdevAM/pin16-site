P0300

Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire Detected

Severity
High
Driving
Short journeys only
System
Powertrain
Group
Ignition system or misfire
Important. A flashing check engine light during a misfire is the one case where continuing to drive risks expensive damage - unburnt fuel reaching the catalytic converter can overheat and destroy it.

The engine is misfiring, but not on one identifiable cylinder - the control module is seeing it randomly or across several at once. Because it is not isolated to a single cylinder, the cause is usually something shared: fuel supply, a vacuum leak, ignition timing, or low compression across the engine rather than one bad part.

Possible causes

  • CommonVacuum leak letting unmetered air into the intake
  • CommonLow or uneven fuel pressure - weak pump or clogged filter
  • CommonWorn spark plugs across the whole engine
  • PossibleFaulty mass airflow sensor giving the module a wrong air reading
  • PossibleEGR valve stuck open, diluting the mixture
  • RareLow compression from worn timing chain or belt

What you might notice

  • Engine shakes or stumbles, worst at idle
  • Loss of power under acceleration
  • Check engine light on, sometimes flashing
  • Higher fuel consumption
  • Rough or unstable idle

What to check, in order

  1. Listen for a hiss around the intake with the engine idling - vacuum leaks are the most common cause of a random misfire.
  2. Check the spark plugs. If they have not been replaced within the service interval, replace them as a set before chasing anything else.
  3. Look at fuel trim on the live data screen. Strongly positive trim points at a vacuum leak or weak fuel delivery.
  4. Check for other stored codes first. A MAF or EGR code alongside this one usually names the real cause.
  5. If the check engine light is FLASHING, stop driving. A flashing light means the misfire is severe enough to damage the catalytic converter.
