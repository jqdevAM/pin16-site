P0298

Engine Oil Over Temperature

Severity
Critical
Driving
Stop driving
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering - injector circuits
Important. If oil temperature is high and oil pressure is falling, that combination is how engines are destroyed. Stopping immediately is far cheaper than any repair that follows from continuing.

The engine oil is too hot. Oil does not just lubricate - it carries heat away from the bearings and the pistons, and above a certain temperature it loses the film strength that keeps metal surfaces apart. Overheated oil leads to bearing damage, and bearing damage leads to an engine rebuild.

Possible causes

  • CommonLow oil level
  • CommonEngine coolant overheating as well, heating the oil with it
  • CommonBlocked or bypassed oil cooler
  • CommonOil of the wrong grade, or long overdue for changing
  • PossibleSustained heavy load - towing, track use, a long climb
  • PossibleFaulty oil temperature sensor
  • PossibleFailing oil pump or a restricted pickup

What you might notice

  • Oil temperature warning
  • Oil pressure warning as the hot oil thins
  • Coolant temperature high as well
  • Loss of power
  • Knocking or rumbling from the engine

What to check, in order

  1. Stop and switch the engine off. Hot oil thins, oil pressure falls, and bearing damage follows quickly.
  2. Check the oil level once the engine has cooled a little - but do not open a hot cooling system while investigating.
  3. Check the coolant temperature as well. Oil and coolant temperatures usually rise together, and the coolant side is the more common origin.
  4. Check the oil cooler and its lines for blockage and leaks.
  5. Check what oil grade was last used and when it was changed.
  6. If the engine was knocking or rumbling before you stopped, do not restart it to move the car. Have it recovered.
