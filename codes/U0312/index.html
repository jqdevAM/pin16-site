U0312

Software Incompatibility with Battery Energy Control Module A

Severity
Critical
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Network and communication
Group
Network software
Important. High-voltage hazard. The orange cables on a hybrid or electric vehicle carry voltages that are lethal on contact, and the system can remain live after the vehicle is switched off. Do not disconnect, cut or probe anything orange, and do not remove high-voltage covers. Reading codes and checking the 12 volt battery are safe; everything past that needs the manufacturer's shutdown procedure and insulated tools.

The software in battery energy control module "A" is not compatible with the rest of the vehicle. This module is part of the high-voltage propulsion system on a hybrid or electric vehicle, and these systems fail safe: when the vehicle cannot confirm that its high-voltage components are correctly matched, it shuts the high-voltage system down. Expect no drive at all, or a hybrid running on the engine alone.

Possible causes

  • CommonBattery energy control module "A" replaced but never programmed or coded to this vehicle
  • CommonUsed module fitted from a different vehicle or battery specification
  • CommonSoftware update interrupted, often by 12 volt battery voltage falling during programming
  • CommonTraction battery pack or module replaced without the matching configuration
  • CommonOne module updated while others were left at their old versions
  • PossibleWrong part number for the vehicle's battery chemistry or capacity

What you might notice

  • Vehicle will not move, or moves only on the engine
  • High-voltage system warning message
  • Vehicle will not charge
  • Traction battery state of charge display blank or implausible
  • Fault present since work on the hybrid system

What to check, in order

  1. Ask what was done. Traction battery, inverter or drive motor work immediately before this appeared is the explanation.
  2. Read the software and part numbers of every high-voltage module and compare them against the vehicle's specification.
  3. Check the 12 volt battery. If a programming session was interrupted, a falling 12 volt supply is usually why - and re-running it needs a battery support unit connected.
  4. Do not replace the module. This is a configuration mismatch, and high-voltage modules are among the most expensive parts on the vehicle to buy twice.
  5. This needs manufacturer-level programming and, for traction battery work, manufacturer-specific configuration.
