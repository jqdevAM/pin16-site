P0563

System Voltage High

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs
Important. A battery being overcharged vents hydrogen, which is explosive, and can become hot enough to distort its case. Do not smoke or work with anything that sparks near it, and do not leave the vehicle running while you investigate. Sealed AGM and lithium batteries tolerate overcharging even less well than a conventional one.

System voltage is too high, measured at the control module's supply. The charging system is pushing more voltage into the vehicle than it should. This is more damaging than it sounds: sustained overcharging boils the battery electrolyte, and the excess voltage stresses every electronic module in the vehicle - which can turn one cheap fault into several expensive ones.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed voltage regulator, usually inside the alternator
  • CommonFailed alternator
  • PossibleFaulty battery temperature sensor, so charging is compensated wrongly
  • PossiblePoor earth between the alternator and the battery, confusing the regulation
  • PossibleControl module commanding the wrong charging voltage
  • PossibleAftermarket or incorrectly specified alternator fitted

What you might notice

  • Headlights unusually bright, and brighter as revs rise
  • Bulbs failing repeatedly
  • Battery warning light on
  • Smell of rotten eggs or acid from the battery
  • Battery case swollen or hot
  • Several unrelated electrical faults appearing at once

What to check, in order

  1. Measure charging voltage at the battery with the engine running and compare it with the manufacturer's specification. A reading well above it confirms overcharging.
  2. Check the alternator earth and the main charging cable connections. A poor earth can make the regulator over-compensate, and that is far cheaper to fix than an alternator.
  3. Check the battery temperature sensor if the vehicle has one - charging voltage is temperature-compensated on most modern vehicles, and a sensor reading wrongly cold makes the system overcharge.
  4. Look at the battery. A swollen case, a hot battery or an acid smell means it is being cooked and should be treated as urgent.
  5. Do not keep driving with a confirmed overcharge. Every mile risks another module.
