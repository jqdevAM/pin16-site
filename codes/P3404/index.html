P3404

Cylinder 1 Deactivation/Intake Valve Control Circuit High

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in

The intake valve control circuit for cylinder 1 is reading high - typically a short to voltage or a lost earth. The module cannot operate the solenoid that deactivates cylinder 1, so that cylinder will stay active. On its own that costs a little fuel economy; the concern is that the same conditions which damage these solenoids also damage the lifters they control.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed intake valve control solenoid on cylinder 1
  • CommonCorroded or oil-contaminated connector at the solenoid
  • CommonWiring damage in the valve cover harness - these run hot and get disturbed during servicing
  • PossibleOil leaking into the connector from a failed seal
  • PossibleBlown fuse on the solenoid supply
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Cylinder deactivation not working, so fuel economy is worse than usual
  • Misfire or rough running when the system tries to switch
  • Ticking noise from the top of the engine
  • Reduced power
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Disconnect the cylinder 1 solenoid and check whether the fault persists at the harness. If it does, the wiring is at fault rather than the solenoid.
  2. Check the engine oil level and condition before anything electrical. This system is actuated by oil pressure through those solenoids, so oil that is low, old or the wrong viscosity causes more faults here than wiring ever does.
  3. Inspect the connector at the cylinder 1 solenoid for oil contamination. Oil wicking into a connector is a common failure on these engines and it looks like an electrical fault.
  4. Measure the solenoid winding resistance against specification.
  5. Read all codes. A misfire code for cylinder 1 alongside this one points at the mechanical side rather than the circuit.
  6. Check the valve cover harness for chafing and heat damage, particularly if the engine has been worked on recently.
