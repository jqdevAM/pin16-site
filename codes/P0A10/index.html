P0A10

DC/DC Converter Status Circuit High Input

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Hybrid propulsion
Important. If the 12 volt system is not being charged, the vehicle will stop when the battery is exhausted - possibly in traffic. Have it recovered rather than driven.

The DC/DC converter status circuit is reading high - typically a short to voltage or a lost earth. The module cannot tell whether the converter is charging the 12 volt system, so it cannot rely on it. On these vehicles the DC/DC converter does the job an alternator does on a conventional car, and nothing else can do it.

Possible causes

  • CommonWiring a short to voltage or a lost earth on the status circuit
  • CommonCorroded or damaged connector
  • CommonFailed DC/DC converter
  • PossibleBlown fuse in the converter's supply or output
  • RareFailed control module input

What you might notice

  • 12 volt battery going flat
  • Multiple unrelated warning lights
  • Vehicle powering down unexpectedly
  • Dim lights, or a battery warning lamp

What to check, in order

  1. Measure the 12 volt system voltage with the vehicle powered up. Around 14 volts means the converter is working and the fault is in the status circuit; around 12 and falling means the converter itself.
  2. Check the converter's fuses on both sides.
  3. Inspect the status circuit connector and wiring for corrosion and damage.
  4. Replace the 12 volt battery only after the converter is confirmed working, not before.
  5. Stop at the 12 volt system. Anything further needs the manufacturer's high-voltage procedure.
