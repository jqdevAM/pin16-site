P0515

Battery Temperature Sensor Circuit

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs

A fault in the battery temperature sensor circuit. Charging voltage is temperature-compensated on most modern vehicles - a cold battery needs a higher charging voltage than a hot one. Without a believable temperature the module has to guess, and a wrong guess means the battery is systematically over- or under-charged.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed battery temperature sensor
  • CommonCorroded connector at the sensor
  • CommonWiring damage - the sensor often sits under or beside the battery
  • PossibleAcid damage to the sensor or its wiring from a leaking battery
  • PossibleSensor not refitted after battery replacement
  • PossibleLoss of reference voltage or earth

What you might notice

  • Battery warning light on
  • Charging voltage slightly wrong
  • Battery life shorter than expected
  • Check engine light on
  • Often no symptom the driver notices

What to check, in order

  1. Ask whether the battery has been replaced recently. The sensor is easily left disconnected or not refitted during a battery change, and that is a very common cause.
  2. Compare the reported battery temperature with ambient on a vehicle that has stood overnight - they should be close.
  3. Inspect the sensor and its wiring for acid damage. A battery that has leaked destroys everything near it.
  4. Check the connector for corrosion.
  5. Check charging voltage against specification while you are there, since this fault affects it directly.
