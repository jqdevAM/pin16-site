U0154

Lost Communication With Restraints Occupant Sensing Control Module

Severity
Critical
Driving
Short journeys only
System
Network and communication
Group
Network communication
Important. Do not work on restraints wiring with the battery connected. Disconnect the battery and wait the period the manufacturer specifies - typically several minutes for the system's reserve energy to drain - before touching any connector. An airbag deploying inadvertently while someone is leaning over it causes serious injury. If you are not confident about this, this is the fault to hand to a workshop.

The control module has stopped receiving messages from the restraints occupant sensing control module, which is part of the airbag and seatbelt pretensioner system. The car drives completely normally and there is nothing to feel - but the restraint system has faulted, and airbags may not deploy in a collision. The airbag warning light will be on, and in most jurisdictions that alone is an inspection failure.

Possible causes

  • CommonLow battery voltage or a discharged battery, dropping modules off the bus
  • CommonConnector under a seat disturbed by seat movement, cleaning or fitting a child seat
  • CommonCorroded or water-ingressed connector - footwell connectors suffer from wet carpets
  • CommonDamaged, chafed or corroded bus wiring
  • PossibleBlown fuse or corroded earth on the supply to the restraints occupant sensing control module
  • PossibleFailed module or sensor
  • PossibleModule not coded or configured after a previous repair or replacement
  • PossiblePrevious accident damage, or a module never replaced after deployment

What you might notice

  • Airbag warning light on and staying on
  • Passenger airbag status indicator wrong or blank
  • Seatbelt warning behaving oddly
  • No effect on how the car drives - this is the point, the fault is silent

What to check, in order

  1. Get this repaired before relying on the car for normal driving. It does not affect control of the vehicle, so short journeys to a garage are reasonable, but the protection you are counting on in a crash is not currently there.
  2. Check battery voltage and state of charge before anything else. A weak or failing battery drops modules off the bus and sets lost-communication codes with no wiring fault at all - this is the single most common false lead in this whole family.
  3. Check whether this code is current or historic. Lost-communication codes are routinely set by a flat battery, a jump start or a battery disconnection, and a historic code straight after one of those events usually records the event rather than a fault.
  4. Ask what happened just before the light appeared. Seats moved, carpets lifted, a child seat fitted or footwell electrics disturbed will account for most of these codes, and the connector under the seat is where to look.
  5. Inspect the yellow restraints connectors for corrosion, water and a locking tab that has not clicked home.
  6. Note how many U-codes are stored. Several at once points at battery, earth or bus wiring, not at several modules failing simultaneously.
  7. Check the module has not simply been left un-coded after a previous repair.
