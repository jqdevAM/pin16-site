P2281

Air Leak Between MAF and Throttle Body

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering - injector circuits

The module has detected an air leak between the mass airflow sensor and the throttle body. That location matters: it has narrowed down where to look, which is the hard part of finding a vacuum leak. Air entering the engine that the module has not accounted for makes the mixture lean, and the module adds fuel to compensate until it runs out of adjustment.

Possible causes

  • CommonSplit or perished intake duct
  • CommonLoose clamp after servicing or intake work
  • CommonLeaking or disconnected breather or PCV hose on that section
  • PossibleCracked resonator or airbox outlet
  • PossibleBoost pipe leak on a turbocharged engine

What you might notice

  • Rough or hunting idle
  • Hesitation or a flat spot under acceleration
  • Fuel trim readings positive - the module adding fuel
  • Worse fuel economy
  • Whistling or hissing from the engine bay
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. This is the most damaging section for a leak. Air entering here has bypassed the mass airflow sensor entirely, so the module has no idea it exists and fuels for less air than the engine is actually getting - which is why the mixture goes lean.
  2. Check every joint and clamp between the sensor and the throttle body, flexing the ducting to find splits that close up at rest.
  3. Watch fuel trim on the live data screen. Strongly positive trim at idle that returns to normal at higher engine speed is the classic signature of a vacuum leak, because the leak matters less when the throttle is open.
  4. Check the PCV system. A perished or disconnected breather hose is a leak, and it is one of the most commonly overlooked.
  5. Do not replace the mass airflow sensor on this code. The sensor is measuring correctly - air is getting past it.
