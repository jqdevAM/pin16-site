P0234

Turbo/Super Charger Overboost Condition

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering - injector circuits
Important. Overboost is one of the few turbo faults that can damage the engine quickly. The reduced-power mode is the protection working; do not try to drive around it.

The turbocharger or supercharger is producing more boost than the module allows. This is a protective code: too much boost raises cylinder pressure beyond what the engine was designed for, and the module cuts power to prevent damage. The engine will drop into a reduced-power mode, often abruptly and often just as you accelerate hard.

Possible causes

  • CommonWastegate stuck closed, or its actuator seized
  • CommonDisconnected or split boost control hose, so the wastegate is never opened
  • CommonFailed wastegate control solenoid
  • CommonVariable-vane mechanism seized with soot on a diesel
  • PossibleFaulty boost sensor over-reporting
  • PossibleAftermarket remap or boost controller raising boost beyond the standard limit

What you might notice

  • Sudden loss of power under hard acceleration
  • Limp mode
  • Loud whistling from the turbo
  • Check engine light on
  • Power returning after a restart

What to check, in order

  1. Be aware that power can be cut abruptly. Avoid overtaking and motorway slip roads until this is fixed - the cut arrives exactly when you ask for full throttle.
  2. Check the boost control hoses for splits and disconnection.
  3. Check the wastegate actuator moves freely, and on a diesel that the variable-vane mechanism is not seized with soot.
  4. Ask whether the vehicle has been remapped or has a boost controller fitted. These raise boost past the module's limit and set this code by design.
  5. Do not keep driving hard with overboost. Sustained excess boost damages pistons, head gaskets and the turbo itself.
