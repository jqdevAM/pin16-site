P2011

Intake Manifold Runner Control Circuit/Open (Bank 2)

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel, air metering and auxiliary emission controls
Important. Inspect the flaps and linkage rather than only clearing the code. On some engines a flap that breaks off can be drawn into a cylinder, and that turns a cheap intake repair into a rebuild. If the linkage feels loose or a flap seems detached, have it looked at before driving further.

A fault in the intake manifold runner control circuit on bank 2 - an open circuit, usually a broken wire or a failed actuator winding. The module cannot operate the runner flaps, so they stay wherever they were and the engine loses part of its power delivery.

Possible causes

  • CommonCarbon build-up seizing the runner flaps or their linkage
  • CommonFailed actuator motor or vacuum diaphragm
  • CommonSplit, perished or disconnected vacuum hose
  • CommonBroken linkage or a detached flap
  • PossibleCorroded connector or damaged wiring
  • PossibleFailed position sensor giving a false reading

What you might notice

  • Loss of low-speed torque or flat top-end power
  • Rough idle
  • Hesitation
  • Worse fuel economy
  • Smoke on a diesel
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Check the vacuum hoses first on a vacuum-operated system - a split or disconnected hose is the most common cause and the cheapest to fix.
  2. Check the flaps and linkage move freely. Carbon seizing them is very common on a diesel used for short journeys, and cleaning is often the whole repair.
  3. Watch commanded against actual runner position on the live data screen through a sweep.
  4. Inspect the connector for corrosion and the actuator for damage.
