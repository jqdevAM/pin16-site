P0620

Generator Control Circuit

Severity
High
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Computer output circuits and control module

A fault in the generator control circuit - the circuit the control module uses to tell the alternator how much to charge. On modern vehicles the alternator is not self-regulating; the module varies charging to suit the battery's state, engine load and fuel economy. Without that control the alternator may charge too much, too little, or not at all.

Possible causes

  • CommonWiring or connector fault between the module and the alternator
  • CommonFailed alternator regulator
  • CommonCorroded connector at the alternator - it sits in a hot, dirty place
  • PossibleChafed harness near the engine
  • PossibleAftermarket or wrong-specification alternator that does not support the control protocol
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Battery warning light on
  • Charging voltage too high or too low
  • Battery going flat, or being overcharged
  • Lights dimming or unusually bright
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Measure charging voltage at the battery and see which way it is wrong. That tells you whether the fault has left the alternator at full output or at none.
  2. Inspect the connector at the alternator for corrosion and heat damage.
  3. Check the wiring between the module and the alternator for chafing.
  4. Ask whether the alternator has been replaced. A unit that does not support the vehicle's control protocol sets this code even though it charges.
  5. Check the earth connections at the alternator and engine.
