P0421

Warm Up Catalyst Efficiency Below Threshold (Bank 1)

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Auxiliary emission controls
Important. A failed converter is usually a symptom rather than a root cause. Something upstream normally killed it - a misfire, a rich mixture or an engine burning oil - and replacing the converter without finding that fault repeats the expense.

The warm-up catalyst on bank 1 is not converting as well as it should. This one sits close to the engine so it reaches working temperature quickly after a cold start, which is when a petrol engine produces most of its emissions - so it does a disproportionate share of the work despite being the smaller unit.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailing or aged downstream oxygen sensor
  • CommonExhaust leak before or between the oxygen sensors
  • CommonEngine fault upstream that has damaged the converter - misfire, rich mixture, oil burning
  • CommonConverter genuinely worn out
  • PossibleOxygen sensor contaminated by oil, coolant or silicone sealant
  • PossibleAftermarket converter that does not meet the original specification

What you might notice

  • Check engine light on
  • Failed emissions test
  • Slight loss of power
  • Sulphurous or rotten-egg smell from the exhaust
  • Rattling from the converter if its internals have broken up
  • Often no symptom the driver notices

What to check, in order

  1. Do not replace the converter first. It is the most expensive item in the exhaust and the oxygen sensors around it fail far more often.
  2. Check for exhaust leaks before and between the sensors. Air drawn in through a leak makes a working converter measure badly.
  3. Watch the upstream and downstream oxygen sensors together on the live data screen. A downstream sensor that mirrors the upstream one suggests the converter really has stopped working; one that sits still suggests the sensor has.
  4. Read all codes. Misfire or fuel trim codes alongside this one identify what damaged the converter, and replacing it without fixing them repeats the expense.
  5. Ask whether an aftermarket converter has been fitted. Cheap replacements frequently fail this test within months even when new.
