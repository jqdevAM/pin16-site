P0650

Malfunction Indicator Lamp (MIL) Control Circuit

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Computer output circuits and control module

A fault in the check engine lamp control circuit. The module cannot control the warning lamp reliably, so it may stay on, stay off, or behave inconsistently. The important case is the lamp that cannot light: the vehicle could store a serious fault and never tell you.

Possible causes

  • CommonBlown indicator bulb
  • CommonWiring or connector fault between the module and the cluster
  • PossibleFailed instrument cluster lamp driver
  • PossibleCorroded connector behind the cluster
  • RareFailed control module output driver

What you might notice

  • Check engine light permanently on with no other codes
  • Check engine light never lighting, including at ignition-on
  • Lamp flickering
  • Emissions test refused

What to check, in order

  1. Watch the lamp at ignition-on. It should light and then go out once the engine starts - that self-test is exactly what an inspection checks for.
  2. A lamp that does not light at all is an inspection failure in most jurisdictions, because the tester cannot confirm the warning system works.
  3. Check the bulb or the cluster segment.
  4. Check the wiring between the module and the cluster.
  5. Treat a permanently lit lamp with no other codes as suspicious - read the codes properly rather than assuming the lamp is at fault.
