P0526

Fan Speed Sensor Circuit

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs
Important. A cooling fan that does not run will overheat the engine in traffic even though it stays cool on the open road. Overheating damage is expensive and quick - watch the temperature gauge until this is fixed.

A fault in the cooling fan speed sensor circuit. This sensor tells the module how fast the cooling fan is actually turning, so the module can confirm the fan responded when it was asked to run.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed fan speed sensor
  • CommonFailed or seizing cooling fan motor
  • CommonCorroded connector or damaged wiring
  • PossibleDebris jamming the fan
  • PossibleFailed fan control module or relay

What you might notice

  • Engine running hotter than usual, particularly in traffic
  • Cooling fan not running, or running constantly
  • Air conditioning performance poor at low speed
  • Temperature gauge climbing when stationary
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Watch the temperature gauge in slow traffic. A cooling fan fault shows itself when the car is stationary and clears at speed, because airflow through the radiator does the fan's job - that pattern is the clearest evidence available.
  2. Check whether the fan runs at all when the engine is hot or the air conditioning is on.
  3. Inspect the connector and wiring at the fan.
  4. Check for debris in the fan and that it spins freely with the engine off and cool.
  5. Do not let the engine overheat while diagnosing this. If the gauge climbs, stop.
