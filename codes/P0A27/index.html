P0A27

Battery Power Off Circuit

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Hybrid propulsion
Important. High-voltage hazard. The orange cables on a hybrid or electric vehicle carry voltages that are lethal on contact, and the system can remain live after the vehicle is switched off. Do not disconnect, cut or probe anything orange, and do not remove high-voltage covers. Reading codes and checking the 12 volt battery are safe; everything past that needs the manufacturer's shutdown procedure and insulated tools.

A fault in the battery power off circuit on a hybrid or electric vehicle. This is the circuit that shuts the high-voltage system down - disconnecting the traction battery when the vehicle is switched off, and in a crash. These systems fail safe, so the usual result is that the high-voltage system will not come on at all.

Possible causes

  • CommonWiring or connector fault in the shutdown circuit
  • CommonLow 12 volt battery voltage - the small battery, not the traction battery
  • PossibleFailed contactor or its control circuit
  • PossibleHigh-voltage interlock loop opened by a disturbed service plug or cover
  • PossibleFailed battery management module

What you might notice

  • Vehicle will not power up or will not move
  • High-voltage system warning message
  • Vehicle will not charge
  • Hybrid running on the engine alone
  • Warning lights for the electric drive system

What to check, in order

  1. Check the 12 volt battery first. On hybrids and electric vehicles a weak 12 volt battery causes a scatter of high-voltage system codes and strands people who assume the expensive traction battery is at fault.
  2. Check whether a service disconnect plug or high-voltage cover has been disturbed. The interlock loop is designed to shut the system down when one is loose.
  3. Read all codes - a battery management module code alongside this one changes the diagnosis.
  4. Read the manufacturer's high-voltage service procedure before going any further.
