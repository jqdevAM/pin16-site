P2783

Torque Converter Temperature Too High

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Transmission
Important. Heat is the main thing that kills automatic transmissions. A single overheating episode shortens fluid life considerably, so a fluid change afterwards is worth doing even if everything seems fine.

The torque converter is running too hot. A torque converter generates heat whenever it is slipping - which is normal at low speed, but sustained under load it produces a great deal of it. Heat is what destroys transmission fluid, and degraded fluid then damages the clutches it should be protecting.

Possible causes

  • CommonTowing or heavy load beyond what the transmission is rated for
  • CommonTorque converter clutch not locking up, so the converter slips constantly
  • CommonLow or degraded transmission fluid
  • CommonBlocked or restricted fluid cooler
  • PossibleProlonged low-speed work - heavy traffic, a long climb, off-road use
  • PossibleFailing converter clutch lining

What you might notice

  • Transmission overheat warning
  • Harsh or delayed shifts
  • Reduced power
  • Burnt smell
  • Transmission slipping

What to check, in order

  1. Stop somewhere safe and let it cool with the engine idling in neutral or park. That circulates fluid through the cooler without adding load and is the quickest way to bring the temperature down.
  2. If you are towing or heavily loaded, that is very likely the cause. Use a lower gear on long climbs so the converter is not slipping.
  3. Read all codes. A converter clutch code alongside this one explains where the heat is coming from.
  4. Check the fluid level and condition once cool. Burnt-smelling fluid means damage has begun.
  5. Check the cooler and its lines for blockage, and the radiator for debris blocking airflow.
