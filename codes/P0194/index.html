P0194

Fuel Rail Pressure Sensor Circuit Intermittent

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering
Important. An engine that cuts out while the car is moving loses steering and brake assistance at the same moment. Do not keep driving it to see whether it happens again. A high pressure fuel system is dangerous to work on. A common rail diesel runs well over 1,500 bar and a direct injection petrol several hundred, and the rail stays pressurised after the engine is switched off. Fuel escaping from a pinhole at those pressures passes straight through skin and injects into the tissue underneath: it can look like a trivial puncture and it is a surgical emergency. Never run a hand along a high pressure line to feel for a leak, never slacken a union with the engine running, and always follow the manufacturer's depressurisation procedure before touching the high pressure side.

The fuel rail pressure signal drops out and returns. Because the module calculates injector opening times from this figure, an intermittent signal makes the engine cut and recover - and an engine that can cut out is a different problem from one that runs badly.

Possible causes

  • CommonLoose, corroded or backed-out pin in the sensor connector
  • CommonChafed wiring that shorts or opens only when the engine moves on its mounts
  • CommonWater or oil that has crept into the connector
  • PossiblePoor earth connection
  • PossibleFailing sensor breaking down when hot

What you might notice

  • Hard starting, or refusing to start at all
  • Loss of power, or dropping into limp mode
  • Rough running, misfiring, or cutting out
  • Smoke from the exhaust
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Wiggle the connector and flex the harness with live data on screen. An intermittent fault that can be reproduced by hand is nearly solved; one that cannot will not be found by guessing.
  2. Open the connector and look for green corrosion, spread terminals or a pin pushed back out of its housing. Spread terminals are common and easy to miss.
  3. Check the sensor's earth. A poor earth produces exactly this pattern and replacing the sensor changes nothing.
  4. Replace the sensor last. Intermittent faults are connections far more often than components, because a component that has failed usually stays failed.
  5. Log rail pressure while driving rather than testing at idle. A fault that only appears under vibration or load will not show itself on a stationary engine.
