P0127

Intake Air Temperature Too High

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering
Important. Detonation damages pistons and bearings. Until this is fixed, keep the load light in hot weather - no towing and no sustained full throttle.

The air entering the engine is hotter than the module will accept. Hot air is thin air: it carries less oxygen for the same volume, so the engine makes less power, and it brings on detonation because the charge is already hot before it is compressed. The module responds by pulling ignition timing, which is why the car feels flat.

Possible causes

  • CommonBlocked, leaking or oil-fouled intercooler on a turbocharged engine
  • CommonHot air being drawn from inside the engine bay because ducting is missing or displaced
  • CommonFailed intake air temperature sensor reading high
  • PossibleHeat soak after idling in traffic on a hot day - sometimes the code is simply true
  • PossibleCooling system fault raising under-bonnet temperature
  • PossibleExhaust leak close to the intake, or a failed heat shield

What you might notice

  • Loss of power, especially in hot weather or after sitting in traffic
  • Pinging or knocking under load
  • Poor fuel economy
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Check that the air intake ducting is complete and properly seated. A duct that has popped off leaves the engine breathing engine-bay air, and this code is often the only sign.
  2. Compare intake air temperature against ambient with the engine cold and standing overnight. They should be within a few degrees; a large gap on a cold engine condemns the sensor.
  3. On a turbocharged engine, inspect the intercooler and its pipework. A blocked core or an oily internal film both cut its ability to shed heat, and a split pipe lets hot charge air escape before it is cooled.
  4. Check the cooling system as well. An engine running hot heats everything under the bonnet, including the air on its way in.
  5. Read the codes for knock. This condition causes detonation, and a knock code stored alongside tells you it is already happening rather than merely likely.
