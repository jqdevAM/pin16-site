P0484

Fan Circuit Over Current

Severity
High
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Auxiliary emission controls
Important. A cooling fan that will not run overheats the engine in traffic even though it stays cool on the open road. Overheating damage is expensive and quick - watch the temperature gauge until this is fixed, and switch the engine off rather than sitting in a jam with the needle climbing.

The cooling fan circuit is drawing more current than it should. Something is making the fan motor work far harder than normal - most often the motor itself failing, or debris jamming the blades. Left alone, this blows fuses and can overheat the wiring.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed fan motor
  • CommonFailed fan relay or control module
  • CommonBlown fuse on the fan circuit
  • CommonCorroded connector or damaged wiring
  • PossibleDebris jamming the fan
  • PossibleCorroded earth at the fan
  • RareFailed control module driver
  • CommonFan motor bearings failing, so the motor draws excessive current
  • CommonDebris jamming or dragging on the fan blades
  • PossibleShorted wiring in the fan circuit

What you might notice

  • Engine running hot in traffic but cool at speed
  • Cooling fan not running, or running constantly
  • Air conditioning performance poor at low speed
  • Temperature gauge climbing when stationary
  • Check engine light on
  • Blown fuses, sometimes repeatedly
  • Noise from the fan
  • Smell of hot electrical insulation

What to check, in order

  1. Check for debris in the fan and that it spins freely by hand with the engine off and cool.
  2. Listen for bearing noise from the fan motor.
  3. If a fuse keeps blowing, find the cause before fitting another one - repeatedly replacing a fuse on an overloaded circuit is how wiring catches fire.
  4. Inspect the wiring for chafing and heat damage.
  5. Watch the temperature gauge in slow traffic and stop if it climbs. A fan fault shows itself when the car is stationary and clears at speed, because airflow through the radiator does the fan's job - that pattern is the clearest evidence available and costs nothing to observe.
  6. Check whether the fan runs at all when the engine is hot or the air conditioning is on.
  7. Check the fuse and swap the relay with an identical one from elsewhere in the box.
  8. Inspect the connector and the fan earth for corrosion.
  9. Check for debris in the fan and that it spins freely with the engine off and cool.
