P2149

Fuel Injector Group "B" Supply Voltage Circuit/Open

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering
Important. Losing a whole group of injectors dumps unburnt fuel into the exhaust. If the check engine light is flashing, continuing to drive can destroy the catalytic converter - which will cost more than the fault itself.

An open circuit or general fault in the supply voltage circuit for fuel injector group "B". Injectors are usually fed in groups from a common supply, so this fault takes out several cylinders at once rather than one. Expect a severe misfire, an engine running on part of its cylinders, or no start at all.

Possible causes

  • CommonBlown fuse on the injector supply
  • CommonFailed relay feeding the injector group
  • CommonBroken or corroded wire in the supply circuit
  • CommonOne injector shorted internally, taking the supply down
  • PossibleCorroded or water-ingressed connector at the injector harness
  • RareFailed control module supply driver

What you might notice

  • Severe misfire, or the engine running on some cylinders only
  • Engine will not start
  • Heavy shaking and loss of power
  • Misfire codes for several cylinders at once
  • Check engine light flashing

What to check, in order

  1. Check the fuses and relays for the injector circuit first. It is the cheapest cause and it accounts for a large share of these faults.
  2. Note which cylinders are misfiring. If they are all on one bank or all in one group, that confirms a shared supply rather than several failed injectors.
  3. Check for battery voltage at the injector connectors with the ignition on.
  4. Measure the injector windings for one that has shorted internally and pulled the supply down.
  5. A flashing check engine light during a severe misfire means unburnt fuel is reaching the catalytic converter. Stop and have it recovered rather than driving on.
