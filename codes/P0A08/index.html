P0A08

DC/DC Converter Status Circuit

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Hybrid propulsion
Important. A vehicle whose DC/DC converter has failed is running everything from the 12 volt battery alone. It will keep going until that battery is exhausted and then stop - possibly in traffic, and with power steering and brake assistance going at the same moment. Do not set off on a journey with this stored.

A fault in the DC/DC converter status circuit. The DC/DC converter is what a hybrid or electric vehicle has instead of an alternator: it steps the traction battery's high voltage down to around 14 volts to run the lights, the computers and everything else on the ordinary 12 volt system, and to keep the 12 volt battery charged. This circuit is how the module knows whether the converter is working. Note the direction of cause and effect here - on most hybrid faults a flat 12 volt battery is the cause, but when the converter fails the flat 12 volt battery is the consequence.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed DC/DC converter
  • CommonCorroded or damaged connector on the status circuit
  • CommonWiring fault between the converter and the module
  • CommonBlown fuse in the converter's supply or output
  • Possible12 volt battery already exhausted, so nothing behaves predictably
  • RareFailed control module input

What you might notice

  • 12 volt battery going flat, especially overnight
  • Warning lights across several unrelated systems
  • Vehicle refusing to power up, or powering down while driving
  • Dim lights, or a battery warning lamp
  • Hybrid or electric drive system warning message

What to check, in order

  1. Measure the 12 volt system voltage with the vehicle powered up and ready. A healthy DC/DC converter holds it at roughly 14 volts; anything near 12 and falling means the converter is not supplying, and the battery is running the car on its own.
  2. Do not simply replace the 12 volt battery and hand the vehicle back. A new battery on a vehicle whose converter has failed will be flat again within days, and the owner will reasonably think the new battery was faulty.
  3. Check the converter's fuses, on both the input and the output side.
  4. Read all codes. Converter faults usually appear alongside other high-voltage system codes, and those narrow it down.
  5. Stop at the 12 volt system and the fuse box. Anything further needs the manufacturer's high-voltage procedure.
