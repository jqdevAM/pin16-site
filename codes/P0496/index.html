P0496

Evaporative Emission System High Purge Flow

Severity
Low
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Auxiliary emission controls
Important. If the charcoal canister has broken up internally, its debris is what jams the purge valve. Replacing the valve alone will not last.

More fuel vapour is flowing into the engine than the module commanded. Almost always this means the purge valve is stuck open, letting vapour in continuously - including at idle and during starting, when the engine cannot cope with the extra fuel.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed purge control valve
  • CommonCorroded connector or damaged wiring
  • CommonSplit, perished or disconnected purge hose
  • CommonValve stuck open by debris from a degraded charcoal canister
  • PossibleBlown fuse on the circuit
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Hard starting immediately after refuelling
  • Rough idle
  • Stalling shortly after starting
  • Fuel smell
  • Failed emissions test
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Check whether the car is hard to start straight after refuelling, and easier to start if you hold a little throttle. That combination points firmly at a purge valve stuck open.
  2. Check the valve holds vacuum with no power applied. A valve that passes air when closed is the fault.
  3. Inspect the charcoal canister. A canister breaking up sends debris into the valve and holds it open, and fitting a new valve without replacing the canister repeats the failure.
  4. Note whether the car is hard to start straight after refuelling. That symptom is close to diagnostic for a purge valve stuck open, and it costs nothing to observe.
  5. Check the purge hoses for splits and disconnection.
  6. Measure the valve winding resistance against specification.
  7. Inspect the connector for corrosion.
  8. Check whether the valve holds vacuum when closed. A valve that leaks with no power applied is stuck open, and that is what causes the hard starting.
