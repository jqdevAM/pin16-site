P0586

Cruise Control Vent Control Circuit/Open

Severity
Low
Driving
No driving restriction
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs

The cruise control vent control circuit is reading an open circuit - typically a broken wire or a failed winding. On a vacuum-operated cruise system, the vacuum solenoid pulls the throttle open and the vent solenoid lets it close again; losing control of either means the system cannot hold a steady speed and will be disabled.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed vacuum or vent solenoid
  • CommonSplit, perished or disconnected vacuum hose
  • CommonCorroded connector or damaged wiring
  • PossibleFailed vacuum supply - a leaking non-return valve or a weak vacuum pump
  • PossibleFailed cruise control servo
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Cruise control will not hold a speed
  • Cruise control drifting or surging
  • Cruise control cancelling on its own
  • Cruise control will not engage
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Check the vacuum hoses first. On a vacuum-operated cruise system a perished or disconnected hose is the most common cause and the cheapest to fix.
  2. Measure the solenoid winding resistance against specification.
  3. Inspect the connector for corrosion.
  4. Check the vacuum supply and its non-return valve.
  5. Confirm ordinary driving is unaffected before deciding how urgent this is.
