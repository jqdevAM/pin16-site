P2170

Exhaust Pressure Regulator Vent Solenoid Control Circuit Low

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering

A fault in the exhaust pressure regulator vent solenoid circuit - a low reading, usually a short to earth or an open circuit. The exhaust pressure regulator deliberately restricts the exhaust to raise back pressure, which warms the engine faster from cold and helps the particulate filter reach the temperature it needs. This solenoid vents the control pressure that operates it.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed vent solenoid
  • CommonCorroded connector or damaged wiring
  • CommonSplit, perished or disconnected vacuum hose
  • CommonCarbon or soot seizing the regulator mechanism
  • PossibleBlown fuse on the circuit
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Reduced power or limp mode
  • Slow to warm up in cold weather
  • Excessive smoke on a diesel
  • Poor cabin heating
  • Particulate filter regeneration problems
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Check the vacuum hoses first on a vacuum-operated system.
  2. Check the regulator mechanism moves freely - soot seizing it is common on a diesel used for short journeys.
  3. Measure the solenoid winding resistance against specification.
  4. Inspect the connector for corrosion and heat damage.
  5. Read all codes - particulate filter codes alongside this one matter more.
