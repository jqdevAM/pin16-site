P2565

Turbocharger Boost Control Position Sensor Circuit High

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs

The boost control position signal is reading high - typically shorted to voltage or with a lost earth.

Possible causes

  • CommonVariable-vane mechanism sticking with soot, on a diesel
  • CommonFailed boost control position sensor
  • CommonCorroded connector or wiring damaged by exhaust heat
  • CommonActuator linkage worn, disconnected or seized
  • PossibleLoss of reference voltage or earth
  • PossibleFailed actuator motor

What you might notice

  • Reduced power or limp mode
  • Poor acceleration
  • Excessive smoke on a diesel
  • Whistling or fluttering from the turbo
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Check the actuator linkage moves freely. On a diesel used mainly for short journeys, soot seizing the variable-vane mechanism is extremely common and cleaning is often the whole repair.
  2. Watch commanded against actual boost control position on the live data screen through a sweep.
  3. Inspect the connector for corrosion and heat damage.
  4. Check the reference voltage and earth at the sensor.
  5. Suspect the turbocharger itself last - it is by far the most expensive item and by far the least likely cause of a position sensor code.
