U0107

Lost Communication With Throttle Actuator Control Module

Severity
Critical
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Network and communication
Group
Network communication
Important. A vehicle that can enter reduced-power mode at any moment is a hazard when joining fast-moving traffic. Drive it to be repaired, not on a journey that depends on it.

The control module has stopped receiving messages from the throttle actuator control module, which operates the electronic throttle. Without it the engine cannot be given more than a fixed minimum opening, so the vehicle will drop into a reduced-power mode. That happens without warning and without regard to what you are doing at the time.

Possible causes

  • CommonLow battery voltage or a discharged battery, dropping modules off the bus
  • CommonBlown fuse or corroded earth on the supply to the throttle actuator control module
  • CommonDamaged, chafed or corroded bus wiring
  • CommonCorroded or water-ingressed connector at the module
  • PossibleFailed module
  • PossibleAnother module faulting and dragging the whole bus down

What you might notice

  • Sudden loss of power - reduced-power or limp-home mode
  • Engine will not rev beyond a low limit
  • Warning light for reduced engine power
  • Engine may not start
  • Rough or unstable idle

What to check, in order

  1. Be aware that power can be cut without warning. Avoid overtaking, motorway slip roads and anything else that depends on the car responding until this is fixed.
  2. Check battery voltage and state of charge before anything else. A weak or failing battery drops modules off the bus and sets lost-communication codes with no wiring fault at all - this is the single most common false lead in this whole family.
  3. Check whether this code is current or historic. Lost-communication codes are routinely set by a flat battery, a jump start or a battery disconnection, and a historic code straight after one of those events usually records the event rather than a fault.
  4. Check the fuses and earth points for the throttle actuator control module.
  5. Note how many U-codes are stored, and look for throttle codes alongside this one. P0120 to P0124 or P2135 stored at the same time usually names the real fault.
  6. Inspect the module connector for corrosion and water ingress.
