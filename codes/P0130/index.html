P0130

O2 Sensor Circuit (Bank 1 Sensor 1)

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering

A general fault on the upstream oxygen sensor for bank 1 - the sensor sitting before the catalytic converter. This is the sensor the engine uses to trim the fuel mixture in real time, so a fault here affects how the engine runs, not just emissions. The module has seen something wrong with the circuit without narrowing it to high, low or slow.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed oxygen sensor
  • CommonDamaged wiring or connector at the sensor
  • PossibleExhaust leak upstream of the sensor
  • PossibleSensor contaminated by oil, coolant or silicone sealant
  • RareControl module fault

What you might notice

  • Check engine light on
  • Worse fuel economy
  • Rough idle or hesitation
  • Failed emissions test

What to check, in order

  1. Inspect the sensor's wiring and connector. Oxygen sensor leads run close to hot exhaust and chafe or melt more often than the sensor itself fails.
  2. Check for exhaust leaks ahead of the sensor. Air drawn in through a leak gives false readings and produces sensor codes on a healthy sensor.
  3. Watch the sensor's voltage on the live data screen with the engine warm. A working upstream sensor should oscillate rapidly rather than sit still.
  4. Check whether other codes are stored. A fuel trim code alongside this one usually means the sensor is reporting a real mixture problem rather than being faulty itself.
