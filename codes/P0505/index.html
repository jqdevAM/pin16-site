P0505

Idle Air Control System

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs

A fault in the idle air control system. At idle the engine needs a carefully metered trickle of air with the throttle closed, and this system provides it. When it stops working properly the idle wanders, hangs high, or drops low enough to stall.

Possible causes

  • CommonCarbon build-up in the idle air passage or on the throttle plate
  • CommonSticking or failed idle air control valve
  • CommonVacuum leak adding uncontrolled air
  • PossibleDamaged wiring or connector at the valve
  • PossibleDirty throttle body on an electronic throttle

What you might notice

  • Idle too high, too low, or hunting up and down
  • Stalling when coming to a stop
  • Rough idle that improves as soon as you touch the throttle
  • Warning light on

What to check, in order

  1. Listen for a vacuum leak at idle - uncontrolled air defeats idle control entirely.
  2. Inspect the throttle body and idle air passage for carbon build-up.
  3. Check the connector and wiring at the idle air control valve.
  4. If you clean the throttle body, check whether the vehicle needs an idle relearn procedure afterwards.
  5. Check for other stored codes - a MAF or vacuum-related code alongside this one usually names the real cause.
