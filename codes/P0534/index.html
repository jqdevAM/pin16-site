P0534

Air Conditioner Refrigerant Charge Loss

Severity
Low
Driving
No driving restriction
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs

The air conditioning system has lost refrigerant. Every system leaks slowly over years, but the module has decided the charge is now low enough to matter - and it will usually stop the compressor engaging, because running a compressor on a low charge starves it of the oil that travels with the refrigerant.

Possible causes

  • CommonSlow leak at a hose, seal or connection
  • CommonLeaking condenser, often from stone damage at the front of the car
  • CommonLeaking compressor shaft seal
  • CommonNatural loss over many years without servicing
  • PossibleLeaking evaporator inside the dashboard
  • PossibleFaulty pressure sensor reporting low charge that is not real

What you might notice

  • Air conditioning blowing warm
  • Compressor not engaging
  • Cooling gradually getting worse over months
  • Oily residue near a pipe or the condenser
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Have the system tested for leaks properly rather than simply recharged. A recharge without a repair is money spent on refrigerant that will escape again.
  2. Look at the condenser at the front of the car for stone damage and corrosion - it is the most exposed part of the system.
  3. Look for oily residue around pipe joints. Refrigerant carries oil, so a leak leaves a mark.
  4. Do not run the air conditioning with a known low charge. The compressor is lubricated by oil carried in the refrigerant, and running it dry is what turns a leak into a compressor.
  5. Run the air conditioning briefly every few weeks even in winter. It keeps the seals lubricated and is the cheapest way to avoid the next leak.
