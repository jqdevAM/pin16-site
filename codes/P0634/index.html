P0634

PCM/ECM/TCM Internal Temperature Too High

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Computer output circuits and control module

A control module is reporting that it is running too hot inside. Modules are designed to operate within a temperature range, and above it they behave unpredictably - so the module will usually reduce what it is doing to protect itself. Something is either heating it or making it work harder than it should.

Possible causes

  • CommonModule mounted where it gets little airflow, or a blocked cooling path
  • CommonA shorted output making the module work far harder than it should
  • CommonCorroded connector causing high resistance and local heating
  • CommonFailed internal temperature sensor
  • PossibleVery high ambient temperature combined with heavy load
  • PossibleGenuine internal module failure

What you might notice

  • Limp mode or reduced power
  • Fault appearing after a period of driving or in hot weather
  • Module resetting
  • Several unrelated codes at once
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Note when it happens. Only in traffic or hot weather points at cooling; at any time points at something making the module work hard.
  2. Read all codes. A shorted output circuit stored alongside this one is very likely the cause - driving a shorted circuit is what heats the module.
  3. Check the module's mounting and its airflow path. Some are fitted with a small duct or heat sink that can be blocked or missing after previous work.
  4. Inspect the connector for corrosion and heat discolouration. High resistance in a connector generates heat exactly where the module can feel it.
  5. Do not fit a replacement module before finding what is heating it - the new one will heat the same way.
