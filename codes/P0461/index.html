P0461

Fuel Level Sensor "A" Circuit Range/Performance

Severity
Low
Driving
Drivable — book it in

Fuel level sensor "A" is answering, but its reading is not plausible - it does not change as fuel is used, or disagrees with a second sender. A worn track inside the sender is the usual cause.

Possible causes

  • CommonWorn sender track - the most common failure on a high-mileage vehicle
  • CommonFailed fuel level sender in the tank
  • CommonCorroded connector at the tank or under the rear seat
  • PossibleDamaged wiring under the vehicle
  • PossibleFloat detached or fuel-logged
  • PossibleLoss of reference voltage or earth

What you might notice

  • Fuel gauge wrong, jumping or stuck
  • Range calculation implausible
  • Low fuel warning at the wrong level
  • Running out of fuel unexpectedly
  • EVAP or misfire monitors not completing
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Watch the reported level while the tank is filled. A reading that jumps or sticks at one point identifies a worn sender track.
  2. Inspect the connector, usually reachable under the rear seat or through a boot floor panel.
  3. Check the reference voltage and earth at the sender.
  4. Note that several self-tests depend on knowing the fuel level - EVAP leak tests will not run on a nearly full or nearly empty tank, and misfire detection is suppressed when the tank is very low. So a fuel level fault stops unrelated monitors completing.
  5. Until it is fixed, work by trip mileage rather than by the gauge. Running a tank very low is hard on the fuel pump, which relies on the fuel around it for cooling.
