P2282

Air Leak Between Throttle Body and Intake Valves

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering - injector circuits

The module has detected an air leak between the throttle body and the intake valves. That location matters: it has narrowed down where to look, which is the hard part of finding a vacuum leak. Air entering the engine that the module has not accounted for makes the mixture lean, and the module adds fuel to compensate until it runs out of adjustment.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed intake manifold gasket
  • CommonLeaking or disconnected vacuum hose from the manifold
  • CommonCracked plastic intake manifold
  • CommonFailed PCV valve or its hose
  • PossibleLeaking brake servo hose or a failed servo diaphragm
  • PossibleInjector seal leaking on a direct-injection engine

What you might notice

  • Rough or hunting idle
  • Hesitation or a flat spot under acceleration
  • Fuel trim readings positive - the module adding fuel
  • Worse fuel economy
  • Whistling or hissing from the engine bay
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Check every vacuum hose leaving the manifold, including the brake servo hose - a leaking servo affects the brakes as well as the mixture.
  2. Inspect a plastic intake manifold for cracks, particularly around the gasket face and any moulded-in vacuum ports.
  3. Watch fuel trim on the live data screen. Strongly positive trim at idle that returns to normal at higher engine speed is the classic signature of a vacuum leak, because the leak matters less when the throttle is open.
  4. Check the PCV system. A perished or disconnected breather hose is a leak, and it is one of the most commonly overlooked.
  5. Do not replace the mass airflow sensor on this code. The sensor is measuring correctly - air is getting past it.
