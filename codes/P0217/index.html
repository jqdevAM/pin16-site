P0217

Engine Coolant Over Temperature Condition

Severity
Critical
Driving
Stop driving
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering - injector circuits
Important. Scald hazard: never open a hot cooling system. And an engine that has overheated badly should be checked for head gasket damage before it is trusted again, even if it seems to run normally once it has cooled.

The engine is overheating. This is one of the few fault codes that describes damage happening right now rather than a component that has failed. An overheating engine warps cylinder heads, destroys head gaskets and can seize outright, and the difference between an inconvenience and a new engine is often a matter of minutes.

Possible causes

  • CommonLow coolant level, usually from a leak
  • CommonFailed thermostat stuck closed
  • CommonCooling fan not running - failed fan, relay, fuse or temperature switch
  • CommonFailed water pump, or a broken belt driving it
  • CommonAir lock in the cooling system after a previous repair
  • PossibleBlocked radiator, externally with debris or internally with deposits
  • PossibleHead gasket failure pressurising the cooling system

What you might notice

  • Temperature gauge in the red
  • Steam from the bonnet
  • Coolant warning light
  • Sweet smell of coolant
  • Loss of power
  • Engine cutting out or refusing to restart

What to check, in order

  1. Stop as soon as it is safe and switch the engine off. Every further minute of running makes the damage worse and more expensive.
  2. Do not open the expansion tank or radiator cap while the engine is hot. The system is pressurised and the coolant is above its normal boiling point - opening it sprays boiling coolant out and causes serious scalds. Wait until it is cold.
  3. Once cool, check the coolant level and look underneath for leaks and for a broken belt.
  4. Check whether the cooling fan runs when the engine is hot. A dead fan produces overheating in traffic while the car stays cool on the motorway - a very recognisable pattern.
  5. Check the top and bottom radiator hoses once warm. A cold bottom hose on a hot engine points at a thermostat stuck closed.
  6. Do not drive it to a garage on the assumption that it will be fine for a few miles. Have it recovered.
