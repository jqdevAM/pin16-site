P2501

Generator Lamp/L-Terminal Circuit High

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Vehicle speed control, idle control and auxiliary inputs

The generator lamp or L-terminal circuit is reading high - typically shorted to voltage or a lost earth. Because this circuit both drives the warning lamp and, on many alternators, provides the initial excitation, the practical effect ranges from a lamp that behaves wrongly to no charging at all.

Possible causes

  • CommonWiring shorted to voltage or a lost earth on the L terminal circuit
  • CommonFailed alternator regulator
  • CommonBlown warning lamp bulb
  • PossibleCorroded connector at the alternator
  • PossibleFailed instrument cluster lamp driver

What you might notice

  • Battery warning light stuck on or never lighting
  • No charging, or charging only at higher engine speeds
  • Battery going flat
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Measure charging voltage at the battery first, so you know whether this is a lamp problem or a charging problem.
  2. Disconnect at the alternator and see whether the high reading persists at the harness.
  3. Check the warning lamp bulb and the bulb check at ignition-on.
  4. Inspect the alternator connector for corrosion and heat damage.
  5. Check continuity between the alternator and the instrument cluster.
