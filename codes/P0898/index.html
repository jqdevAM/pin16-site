P0898

Transmission Control System MIL Request Circuit Low

Severity
Low
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Transmission

A fault in the circuit the transmission module uses to request the check engine light - a low reading, usually a short to earth or an open circuit. This is about the request wire itself, not about the transmission. The practical consequence is that the transmission module may not be able to warn you: a genuine transmission fault could be stored without the light ever coming on.

Possible causes

  • CommonWiring fault between the transmission module and the module that drives the lamp
  • CommonCorroded or damaged connector at either module
  • PossibleChafed harness shorting against the body or engine
  • PossibleFailed instrument cluster lamp driver on vehicles wired that way
  • RareFailed transmission control module output

What you might notice

  • Check engine light on, or conversely never coming on when it should
  • Warning lamp behaving inconsistently
  • Usually no effect on how the car drives

What to check, in order

  1. Read the transmission module for its own stored codes. The point of this circuit is to warn you about those, so check what it was trying to report.
  2. Inspect the connectors at both ends of the circuit.
  3. Follow the wire looking for chafe damage.
  4. Confirm the warning lamp itself works at ignition-on bulb check.
  5. Treat this as a warning system fault rather than a transmission fault - but do not ignore it, because it means the car's ability to tell you about transmission problems is compromised.
