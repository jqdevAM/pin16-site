P0335

Crankshaft Position Sensor "A" Circuit

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Ignition system or misfire

A fault in the crankshaft position sensor "A" circuit. This sensor tells the module where the crankshaft is and how fast it is turning, and the module uses it to decide when to fire each spark plug and injector. It is the one sensor an engine genuinely cannot run without.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed crankshaft position sensor - very often one that works cold and fails hot
  • CommonCorroded, oil-contaminated or damaged connector at the sensor
  • CommonChafed or heat-damaged wiring - the sensor sits low on a hot engine
  • PossibleMetal debris on the sensor tip
  • PossibleDamaged, loose or misaligned reluctor ring on the crankshaft
  • PossibleStretched timing chain or slipped belt changing the signal relationship

What you might notice

  • Engine cuts out while driving and will not restart until it cools
  • Long cranking, or no start at all
  • Rev counter dead or erratic while cranking
  • Stalling at idle
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Note whether the fault is heat-related. A sensor that works from cold, fails after twenty minutes and works again an hour later is the classic crankshaft sensor failure, and that pattern is worth more than any measurement.
  2. Inspect the connector for oil contamination and corrosion. These sensors sit low on the engine where oil leaks find them.
  3. Check the wiring for chafe and heat damage along its run.
  4. Inspect the sensor tip for metal debris.
  5. Watch engine speed on the live data screen while cranking. No reading confirms the signal is genuinely absent rather than merely poor.
