P2029

Fuel Fired Heater Disabled

Severity
Low
Driving
No driving restriction
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel, air metering and auxiliary emission controls

The fuel-fired heater has been disabled. On some diesels an auxiliary burner heats the coolant so the cabin warms up quickly and the engine reaches temperature sooner - a diesel produces little waste heat when cold. Being disabled usually means the module has switched it off deliberately rather than that it has broken.

Possible causes

  • CommonFuel fired heater disabled by the module because another fault is present
  • CommonBlocked or sooted burner from repeated short runs
  • CommonFailed glow pin or igniter inside the heater
  • CommonFuel supply problem to the heater
  • PossibleBlocked exhaust or air intake for the heater
  • PossibleLow battery voltage - these heaters draw heavily on start-up

What you might notice

  • Auxiliary heater not working
  • Cabin slow to warm in cold weather
  • Engine slow to reach operating temperature on a diesel
  • Heater warning message
  • No symptom in warm weather

What to check, in order

  1. Check the battery. These heaters draw a large current when starting and will refuse to run on a weak battery - which is also exactly when you want them.
  2. Read all codes. The heater is often disabled because of another fault rather than because it has failed.
  3. Check the heater's own exhaust and air intake for blockage.
  4. Ask how it is used. Repeated very short runs soot the burner up, and many of these heaters have a service routine to clear it.
  5. Check the fuel supply to the heater, which is usually a separate pump and line from the tank.
