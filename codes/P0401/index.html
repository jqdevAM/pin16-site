P0401

Exhaust Gas Recirculation Flow Insufficient Detected

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Auxiliary emission controls
Important. Do not soak an electronically actuated EGR valve in solvent with its motor and position sensor still attached - the cleaner destroys them. Either remove the actuator first or replace the valve as a unit. This warning does not apply to a plain vacuum-operated valve, which can be cleaned freely.

The module commanded exhaust gas recirculation and measured less flow than it expected, or none at all. This is the single most common EGR fault, and on a high-mileage engine the cause is almost always carbon - the recirculated exhaust gas deposits soot, and over years that soot narrows or blocks the passages the gas has to travel through.

Possible causes

  • CommonCarbon blocking the EGR passage in the intake manifold or cylinder head
  • CommonEGR valve stuck shut by carbon on its seat
  • CommonFaulty differential pressure sensor (the DPFE sensor on many Ford engines)
  • PossibleBlocked or split hoses feeding the differential pressure sensor
  • PossibleNo vacuum reaching a vacuum-operated valve - split hose or failed solenoid
  • PossibleClogged EGR cooler on a diesel engine
  • PossibleFailed EGR valve motor on an electronically actuated valve

What you might notice

  • Check engine light on
  • Failed emissions test - nitrogen oxide readings high
  • Pinging or knocking under load, especially climbing a hill
  • Slight loss of power
  • Frequently no symptom the driver notices at all

What to check, in order

  1. Remove the EGR valve and look at it, and look into the passage behind it. If either is packed with soot, that is the fault - no further diagnosis is needed before cleaning.
  2. Clean the valve and the passage with carbon cleaner and a suitable brush. Blocked passages are frequently the whole repair, and a new valve fitted onto a blocked passage will set the same code again within days.
  3. Check the small hoses to the differential pressure sensor. They run close to hot components, go brittle and split, and a split hose produces this code with a perfectly good valve.
  4. On a vacuum-operated system, apply vacuum to the valve directly with a hand pump and watch it open. If it holds vacuum and moves, the valve is fine and the fault is upstream in the control solenoid or hoses.
  5. On an electronically actuated valve, compare commanded against actual position on the live data screen while the engine warms up.
  6. On a diesel, consider the EGR cooler. Coolers block with soot in the same way the valve does, and a blocked cooler restricts flow no matter how well the valve works.
