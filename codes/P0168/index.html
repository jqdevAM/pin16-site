P0168

Fuel Temperature Too High

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering

The fuel is hotter than the module will accept. Fuel returning from the injection system carries heat back to the tank, and the fuel in the tank is what absorbs it. Hot fuel is less dense, so the same injector opening time delivers less energy - and on a diesel it also lubricates less well.

Possible causes

  • CommonRunning the tank persistently low, so there is little fuel to absorb the returning heat
  • CommonBlocked or damaged fuel cooler, where one is fitted
  • CommonRestricted return line
  • CommonFailed fuel temperature sensor reading high
  • PossibleSustained heavy work in hot weather - towing, or long climbs
  • PossibleExcessive injector leak-off returning large quantities of hot fuel

What you might notice

  • Loss of power when hot, recovering after the engine cools
  • Poor fuel economy
  • Rough running when the tank is low
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Keep the tank fuller. On vehicles that return a lot of fuel this alone can stop the code, and it is worth trying before anything is dismantled.
  2. Check the fuel temperature reading on live data with the engine cold. It should sit close to ambient; a cold engine reporting hot fuel is a sensor fault.
  3. Inspect the fuel cooler and its airflow if one is fitted. Road debris packed into the fins stops it working.
  4. Check the return line for kinks or restriction, particularly after any work in that area.
  5. Consider injector leak-off. Worn injectors return a great deal of fuel, and that fuel is hot - so this code and a fuel delivery code together point the same way.
