P2418

Evaporative Emission System Switching Valve Control Circuit/Open

Severity
Low
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Auxiliary emission controls

The control circuit for the evaporative emission switching valve is open - a broken wire, a disconnected plug or a failed valve winding. This valve directs where the leak detection pump sends its air - through the system under test, or through a calibrated reference orifice the module uses to know what a genuine small leak looks like. Without it the leak test has nothing to compare against and cannot run.

Possible causes

  • CommonCorroded or water-damaged connector at the valve
  • CommonFailed valve winding
  • CommonDamaged underbody wiring
  • PossibleBlown fuse in the shared EVAP supply
  • RareFailed driver inside the control module

What you might notice

  • Check engine light on
  • Failed emissions test
  • Occasionally a smell of fuel
  • Usually nothing else - EVAP faults rarely affect how the car drives

What to check, in order

  1. Inspect the connector and its wiring before condemning the component. Everything in this system lives under the vehicle in the path of road salt and spray, and a corroded connector produces these codes far more often than a failed part does.
  2. Measure the valve winding's resistance against specification. Open or shorted is conclusive.
  3. Check whether other EVAP components report faults at the same time. Several at once points at the shared fuse, feed or earth rather than at several failed valves.
  4. Command the valve with a scan tool and listen for it to click.
