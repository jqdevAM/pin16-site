P0149

Fuel Timing Error

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering
Important. On a common rail engine, do not slacken any union on the high pressure side to look for a problem. That side stays pressurised after the engine stops, and fuel escaping at those pressures will pass through skin. Follow the manufacturer's depressurisation procedure.

Injection is not happening when the module intended. On a diesel, when fuel is injected relative to piston position determines how it burns - too early and combustion is harsh and noisy, too late and it is smoky and inefficient. The module compares what it commanded against what it observes and sets this when they diverge.

Possible causes

  • CommonLow fuel pressure making injection slower than the module allowed for
  • CommonFault in the timing control or metering valve
  • CommonWorn injectors responding sluggishly to their commands
  • PossibleCrankshaft and camshaft position signals out of correlation
  • PossibleTiming belt or chain that has jumped a tooth
  • PossibleInjection pump timing incorrect after previous work

What you might notice

  • Harsh diesel knock, or unusually noisy running
  • Loss of power and limp mode
  • White, black or blue smoke
  • Hard starting
  • Poor fuel economy

What to check, in order

  1. Read all codes first. Crankshaft or camshaft position codes stored alongside change the answer completely - fix those before looking at anything in the fuel system.
  2. Check fuel pressure against the commanded value. Timing errors are frequently pressure problems in disguise, because injection duration depends on pressure.
  3. Check the cam and crank correlation. A timing belt or chain that has jumped will produce this and is worth ruling out early, because continuing to run the engine makes it worse.
  4. Confirm injection pump timing if the pump or belt has recently been disturbed. This code appearing shortly after belt work usually points straight back at it.
