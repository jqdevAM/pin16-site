P0218

Transmission Fluid Over Temperature Condition

Severity
High
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering - injector circuits
Important. Heat is the main thing that kills automatic transmissions. A single overheating episode shortens fluid life considerably, so a fluid change afterwards is worth doing even if everything seems fine.

The transmission fluid is too hot. Heat is what destroys automatic transmission fluid, and degraded fluid then damages the clutches it is supposed to protect. Most transmissions will reduce what they allow you to do while this is happening, which is the protection working.

Possible causes

  • CommonTowing or heavy load beyond what the transmission is rated for
  • CommonLow or degraded transmission fluid
  • CommonBlocked or restricted transmission fluid cooler
  • CommonTorque converter clutch not locking up, so the converter generates heat
  • PossibleSlipping clutches inside the transmission
  • PossibleEngine cooling system fault, since many coolers run through the radiator
  • PossibleFaulty temperature sensor

What you might notice

  • Transmission warning message
  • Harsh or delayed shifts
  • Reduced power
  • Burnt smell
  • Transmission slipping

What to check, in order

  1. Stop somewhere safe and let it cool with the engine idling in neutral or park. That circulates fluid through the cooler without adding load, and it is the quickest way to bring the temperature down.
  2. If you are towing or heavily loaded, that is very likely the cause. Reduce the load, and use a lower gear on long climbs so the torque converter is not slipping.
  3. Check the transmission fluid level and condition once cool. Burnt-smelling fluid means damage has already begun.
  4. Check the transmission cooler and its lines for blockage, and the radiator for debris blocking airflow.
  5. Read all codes. A torque converter clutch code alongside this one explains where the heat is coming from.
