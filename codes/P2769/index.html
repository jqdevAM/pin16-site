P2769

Torque Converter Clutch Circuit Low

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Transmission

The torque converter clutch circuit is reading low - typically shorted to earth or an open circuit. The converter clutch cannot be controlled, so it will not lock at cruising speed. The car drives normally but the engine sits at higher revs on the motorway and uses noticeably more fuel.

Possible causes

  • CommonFailed torque converter clutch pressure control solenoid
  • CommonLow or degraded transmission fluid
  • CommonDebris in the fluid jamming the solenoid or a valve
  • CommonCorroded case connector, or fluid wicking into it
  • CommonWorn torque converter clutch lining
  • PossibleInternal transmission harness failure

What you might notice

  • Shudder or vibration at a light steady throttle
  • Higher engine speed at a steady cruise than usual
  • Worse fuel consumption
  • Transmission running hot
  • Stalling when coming to a stop
  • Check engine light on

What to check, in order

  1. Disconnect at the case connector and check whether the low reading persists at the harness side.
  2. Pull the transmission case connector and look for fluid inside it. Fluid wicking up the harness corrodes the pins and can reach and destroy the control module itself.
  3. Check the fluid level and condition first. It is the cheapest check and it explains more transmission complaints than every electrical fault combined.
  4. A converter clutch that never locks makes the transmission run hotter, which shortens fluid life. Worth fixing even though nothing feels wrong.
