P2785

Clutch Actuator Temperature Too High

Severity
Medium
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Transmission

The clutch actuator on an automated manual transmission is running too hot. These actuators are electric motors doing a job a driver's leg or arm would otherwise do, and working them continuously - in stop-start traffic, or holding the car on a slope - heats them beyond what they are designed for. The transmission will restrict itself until they cool.

Possible causes

  • CommonProlonged stop-start traffic or repeated hill starts
  • CommonHolding the vehicle on a slope using the transmission rather than the brakes
  • CommonWorn clutch making the actuator work harder than it should
  • PossibleSticking or dry actuator mechanism
  • PossibleLow hydraulic pressure making the actuator work continuously
  • PossibleFailed cooling for the actuator on designs that have it

What you might notice

  • Transmission warning message
  • Reduced power or refusal to move
  • Fault appearing in heavy traffic or on a hill
  • Judder when moving off
  • Recovering after the car has stood for a while

What to check, in order

  1. Stop and let it cool. These faults clear themselves once the temperature falls, and forcing the issue is what turns overheating into a failed actuator.
  2. Hold the car on the footbrake or handbrake on a slope rather than on the transmission. This is the single most effective habit change on an automated manual, and it costs nothing.
  3. Assess clutch wear. A worn clutch makes the actuator work far harder for every engagement.
  4. Read all codes - hydraulic pressure codes alongside these explain an actuator working continuously.
  5. Check whether the clutch adaptation procedure has been run since the last repair.
