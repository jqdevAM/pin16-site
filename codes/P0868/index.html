P0868

Transmission Fluid Pressure Low

Severity
Critical
Driving
Stop driving
System
Powertrain
Group
Transmission
Important. This is the transmission code most worth acting on immediately. Low line pressure destroys clutch packs quickly, and the cost difference between stopping now and driving on is usually the difference between a solenoid or a fluid leak and a full rebuild.

Transmission fluid pressure is low. This matters more than most transmission codes: line pressure is what clamps the clutch packs together, and a clutch that is clamped weakly slips. A slipping clutch generates heat, and heat destroys the friction material quickly - a transmission driven for an hour with low pressure can need a rebuild that it would not have needed if it had been stopped.

Possible causes

  • CommonLow fluid level - the cheapest and most common cause
  • CommonExternal fluid leak
  • CommonBlocked transmission filter
  • CommonFailed pressure control solenoid
  • PossibleWorn pump
  • PossibleInternal leak past worn seals or a cracked drum
  • PossibleFaulty pressure sensor reporting low when pressure is fine

What you might notice

  • Slipping - engine speed rises without the car accelerating
  • Delayed or no engagement when selecting drive or reverse
  • Harsh, flaring shifts
  • Limp mode
  • Burnt smell
  • Fluid puddle under the car

What to check, in order

  1. Stop driving and check the fluid level. Continuing to drive with genuinely low pressure is the difference between a repair and a rebuild, and the damage accumulates within minutes rather than weeks.
  2. Look underneath for a leak. A sudden low-pressure fault on a transmission that was fine yesterday is usually a leak, and topping up will get you home.
  3. Check the fluid condition. Burnt, dark fluid means damage has already begun.
  4. If the level is correct, do not keep driving on the assumption that the sensor is lying. Have the pressure tested with a gauge.
  5. Read all codes. Ratio or slip codes alongside this one confirm the clutches really are slipping.
