P2102

Throttle Actuator Control Motor Circuit Low

Severity
Critical
Driving
Drive only to a garage
System
Powertrain
Group
Fuel and air metering
Important. A vehicle that can enter reduced-power mode at any moment is a hazard when joining fast-moving traffic. Drive it to be repaired, not on a journey that depends on it.

The throttle actuator motor circuit is reading low - typically shorted to earth or an open circuit. The module cannot control the throttle, so the engine drops into a reduced-power mode.

Possible causes

  • CommonCarbon build-up making the throttle plate stiff
  • CommonFailed throttle actuator motor
  • CommonCorroded or damaged connector at the throttle body
  • CommonWiring damage between the module and the throttle body
  • CommonThrottle body never relearned after cleaning or replacement
  • RareFailed control module driver

What you might notice

  • Sudden loss of power - reduced-power mode
  • Engine will not rev beyond a low limit
  • Rough or unstable idle
  • Warning light for reduced engine power
  • Engine may not start

What to check, in order

  1. Disconnect the throttle body and check whether the low reading persists at the harness. If it does, the fault is in the wiring rather than the actuator.
  2. Be aware that power can be cut without warning. Avoid overtaking and motorway slip roads until this is fixed.
  3. Inspect the throttle body for carbon. On a drive-by-wire throttle, cleaning without running the relearn procedure afterwards can leave the idle worse than before - check whether a relearn is specified before starting.
  4. Compare commanded against actual throttle position on the live data screen through a full sweep.
  5. Inspect the connector at the throttle body for corrosion.
  6. Measure the actuator motor resistance against specification.
