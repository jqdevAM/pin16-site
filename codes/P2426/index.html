P2426

Exhaust Gas Recirculation Cooling Valve Control Circuit Low

Severity
Low
Driving
Drivable — book it in
System
Powertrain
Group
Auxiliary emission controls

A fault in the control circuit for the EGR cooling valve - low, usually a short to earth or an open circuit. The EGR cooler passes recirculated exhaust gas through engine coolant to cool it before it reaches the intake; this valve controls that cooling. The fault is electrical, in the circuit that operates the valve.

Possible causes

  • CommonCorroded or damaged connector at the EGR cooling valve
  • CommonFailed cooling valve solenoid or actuator
  • PossibleWiring fault between the module and the valve
  • PossibleChafed harness near the exhaust or cooler
  • RareFaulty control module driver

What you might notice

  • Check engine light on
  • Failed emissions test
  • Increased exhaust smoke on a diesel in some cases
  • Usually no symptom the driver notices

What to check, in order

  1. Inspect the connector at the cooling valve. It sits in a hot area and corroded or heat-damaged connectors are the usual cause.
  2. Measure the actuator winding resistance against specification.
  3. Check the wiring for chafe damage where the harness runs near the cooler or exhaust.
  4. Check for supply voltage at the valve with the ignition on.
  5. Inspect the cooler itself for coolant leaks while you are there. A leaking EGR cooler shows as white smoke and unexplained coolant loss, and is a different and more serious problem than this circuit fault.
